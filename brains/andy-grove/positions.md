# Andy Grove: positions

Where Grove stands on specific questions, grouped by topic. Dates come from the source date. His sources run from 1987 (S013) to 2010 (S008). His latest positions are on jobs and manufacturing (2010, S008), batteries and industrial policy (2009, S009), and health-care reform (2007, S006). S010 to S013 are journalist pieces: only his quoted words are his. S005 is a summary made from his speaker notes, so its wording is lower-confidence.

## Management and organizations

**Judge managers by output, not by traits (1987).** "Effective managers are distinguished by what they accomplish, not by what they are." [S013] There is no magic list of qualities. "I advise aspiring managers to stop worrying about what magic characteristics they need; instead I urge them to concentrate on the task at hand and use their own strengths and capabilities to the best effect" [S013]

**An organization with no expectations produces nothing (recalled 2004).** On the Fairchild research lab: "I guess no pressure because very little was expected in terms of tangible output." [S001] "There was no internal discipline to the place; and there was no external discipline or expectations that were put on the lab" [S001] Its service lab "took forever to do their job, picked their own priorities." [S001]

**Inaction by leaders is the main management failure (2004).** "The reason Fairchild disintegrated was because neither Bob nor Gordon was capable of removing a Tom Bay" [S001] On Moore: "every trouble or any misgiving I have ever had about Gordon was a case of inaction." [S001] Someone must play the enforcer across boundaries: "In this context, I think my role at Intel was the Charlie Sporck role. It worked." [S001]

**Do not separate development from production (2004).** "For example, we didn’t have separate R&D. We still don’t have separate R&D." [S001] At Fairchild, handing work across organizational lines failed: "For all practical purposes, everybody involved had given up on that." [S001] At Intel, "The whole transfer issue had the technical difficulties but it didn’t have the organizational difficulties." [S001]

**The work happens at the working level, not in middle layers (2004).** "The department heads in my opinion were useless, without a single exception." [S001] On section heads: "that’s where the work was being done." [S001]

**Every yes needs a no (1998).** "And everybody is very eager to give you advice how to put yeses to the balance sheet and nobody ever volunteers how to put nos." [S003] Any organization is finite (inferred from S003).

**Find the real bottleneck in the system (1996 to 2010).** "Processor performance is only as useful as the I/O allows it to be." [S004] "we recognized that performance improvement was not limited by the silicon, but rather by the single instruction path." [S005] He applies the same move to whole economies (see industrial policy).

**Data decides arguments, but data only describes the past (1998).** "Don't argue with the emotions, argue with the data." [S003] Yet "the data that deal with your business are pertinent to your past, and don't say anything about your future." [S003] And "You can always hide behind the data." [S003] This is a refinement, not a reversal, of his 1996 stance as a patient: "I fell back on my data. I looked at my plots." [S007]

**Listen to bad news and remove fear (1998).** "It is extremely important to be able to listen to the people who bring you bad news" [S003] "Unless you deal with this fear, unless you live this fear you will never hear from those helpful Cassandras and you are going to be late in responding to the Strategic Inflection Points." [S003] Senior people see change last: "those of us in senior management are among the last ones to notice." [S003]

**Act before you announce (1998).** "It is important to put the actions in place before you start talking about it because talking prematurely about changes disrupts peoples' lives, existence, employment" [S003]

**Strategic Inflection Points demand fundamental change (1997 to 1998).** "A Strategic Inflection Point is that which causes you to make a fundamental change in business strategy. Nothing less is sufficient." [S003] Once committed, "the course that you've committed to tend to be very difficult to reverse." [S004]

**Cheap and good enough beats superior (1998).** "It doesn't matter how inferior the new approach is. What matters is, is the new approach is good enough for the market?" [S003] "Not everything that's low cost is a Segment Zero." [S003] On pricing: "you must price the market and cost must follow price." [S003]

**Ethics are personal, not waiting on the top (1987).** "I do believe that ethics start at the top, but I don't advocate it because it gives a cop-out for everybody to wait until the old man up there starts handing down ethical principles" [S013]

## Governance and boards

**Documented change of mind.** He says he had "gotten religion" [S010] about governance after becoming chairman in 1997. By 2004 his own behavior was the main fix: "The most important tinkering I had to do was with me." [S010] "Arthur was really telling me that if I wanted the board to be more helpful, I would have to learn how to shut up." [S010]

**The board exists to outlast any one leader (2004).** "So you’re looking for the board to act as a gyroscope, to stabilize the company over time." [S010] "Continuity is the key" [S010]

**Directors must engage in strategy and own outcomes (2004).** "After all, good strategy is good governance." [S010] He supports Sarbanes-Oxley and would go further: "We should require directors to attest to a certain level of knowledge and involvement in strategic decisions" [S010] "For them to own a piece of the action, they need to be visibly associated with the outcome" [S010]

**Review the chief executive openly (2004).** "We’ll be doing Craig’s performance review, which should be interesting because we’re doing it right in front of him." [S010]

**Board structure (2004, journalist's account, S010).** Independent directors as a clear majority. Separate CEO and chairman roles. Compliance pushed to committees so the full board can vet strategy. Directors take technical seminars and visit at least one site a year. Retirement at 72.

## Health care and medicine

**1996: the patient must be the analyst.** Specialists favor their own tools: "urologists have a natural preference toward surgery, perhaps because urologists are surgeons and surgery is what they know best." [S007] "There is no good gatekeeper in this business." [S007] So "I concluded that I had to undertake the generalist’s job myself" [S007]

**1996: compare outcomes across all treatments.** "The right answer, in my view, can be arrived at only by comparing the results achieved by the practitioners of all the different treatment forms." [S007] Medicine fell short of engineering here: "Medical practitioners primarily tended to publish their own data; they often didn’t compare their data with the data of other practitioners, even in their own field" [S007] Outcome tables "should be viewed as the point of departure for a prostate cancer patient’s bill of rights." [S007]

**1996: better measurement speeds learning, so screen.** "The most important thing I learned was that the use of the PSA test reset the entire field of prostate cancer studies." [S007] "Remember, it’s a marker. What PSA gives you is the chance to act early. Don’t blow it." [S007] Treat early and hard: "I think you should hit a tumor with what you believe is your best shot, early and hard." [S007]

**1996: verify the measurement itself.** "So I also decided to test the tests." [S007]

**1998: medicine is at a Strategic Inflection Point driven by patients.** "consumers of health care services are ahead of the profession in their embrace of electronic means of getting information" [S002] "So if nothing else, self-defense compels you to take some action." [S002] The bottleneck is providers: "it requires the willing and enthusiastic participation of the health care providers as part of the network. And the picture here is quite mixed." [S002] Fears of e-mail overload are overblown (inferred from S002). "the water is surrounding the castle here." [S002] He asks for outcome evidence on any intervention: "Have you studied the impact of these electronic discussion groups on their prognosis and the well-being of the patients?" [S002]

**2005: move fast, challenge imagined obstacles.** To Intel's health-care staff: "be quick and dirty" [S012] On a privacy objection: "Can I ask you a question? Why do you care?" [S012] "I think we have a tendency toward adding imaginary complexities to a problem which is already unimaginably complicated." [S012]

**2007 (latest): fix two specific things, not the whole system.** "Fix specific problems of the system. Leave bigtime changes for later, after you've demonstrated that you can solve more limited problems." [S006] "I propose that you commit to two - and only two - programs." [S006] Even his own grand plan is too much: "The only problem with my plan - and yours - is that it's too much." [S006] "Society is simply resistant to fundamental change. Dramatic departures can take place only in a crisis." [S006] His ideal design covers preventive and catastrophic care publicly (inferred from S006).

This is a shift in method from 1996 to 2007. In 1996 he urged aggressive, early action on a tumor. In 2007 he urged small, proven steps on a whole system (inferred from S007 and S006).

**2007: emergency rooms need standards and a clinic next door.** "Emergency rooms have become the de facto health-care system for millions." [S006] "The first step in fixing this mess is to establish minimum standards for all ERs." [S006] "The same goes for acceptable patient waiting times: Set the standard, and implement it." [S006] "Require a triage system that directs patients to either the ER or the clinic, using well-defined criteria." [S006] "Running the clinics could even be outsourced." [S006] Funding comes from a 1% surtax pool, paid only to ERs that qualify (inferred from S006).

**2007: keep the elderly at home with cheap sensors.** "So it stands to reason that if there were a way to keep elderly patients in their own homes longer - without degrading quality of care - we'd have a cheaper and better system." [S006] "Not to spy, but to detect trouble." [S006]

**2007: records should be simple and portable.** "Unlike the task of harmonizing the many assorted electronic medical-record systems used in hospitals, this is a technically trivial job if you act now." [S006]

**2007: do the math, then commit.** "Can we afford all this? Let's do the math." [S006] "Not making these reforms would be the same as burning $30 million a day at the local dump." [S006] "Commit to doing these two concrete things - now." [S006]

## Technology and the internet

**1997: e-commerce is a Strategic Inflection Point.** "Transactions, business will be connected not face to face but stream to stream." [S004]

**1998: technology will arrive regardless.** "Anything that can be done in technology will be done." [S002]

## Industrial policy and jobs (his latest, 2009 to 2010)

**Startups do not scale employment on their own (2010).** "Friedman is wrong." [S008] "Startups are a wonderful thing, but they cannot by themselves increase tech employment." [S008] "Scaling is hard work but necessary to make innovation matter." [S008]

**Losing manufacturing breaks the learning chain (2010).** "Not only did we lose an untold number of jobs, we broke the chain of experience that is so important in technological evolution." [S008] Optimizing one metric hurts the system: "If profit margins are the problem, we go to work on margins, with exquisite focus." [S008] "Simply put, the U.S. has become wildly inefficient at creating American tech jobs." [S008]

**Jobs come first, even if it looks protectionist (2010).** "Unemployment is corrosive. If what I’m suggesting sounds protectionist, so be it." [S008] Ring-fence any levy: "Keep that money separate." [S008]

**Capacity must be built before demand is proven (2010).** "The decision to build these plants needed to be made years before we knew whether the Pentium chip would work or whether the market would be interested in it." [S008]

**Government should lead, then leave (2009).** The bottleneck for electric vehicles: "But the critical limitation is going to be battery production." [S009] A public battery venture should then "license the manufacturing technology to private companies and let it go out of business." [S009] "We can do the same for transportation. We must." [S009]
