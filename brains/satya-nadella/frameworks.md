# Satya Nadella: Frameworks

Pillar: Transformation and change adoption.
Core question: Will the people actually adopt this, and does it make the institution a learn-it-all?

---

## 1. Refounding: mission and culture first, then strategy

A non-founder CEO must make explicit what founders carry by instinct. He grounds the institution again in why it exists. Only then does he pursue a bold new strategy. Mission and culture are preconditions. They do not replace good strategy or execution (inferred from S010, S011).

- "from time to time, companies need that moment where you need to reground yourself" [S009]
- "I felt that we needed to essentially refound the company, ground ourselves back in what's the core sense of purpose and mission so that then we can pursue something new and bold" [S010]
- "they’re necessary but not sufficient because then you’ve got to get your strategy right and execution right, and you’ve got to adapt because everything in life is path dependent" [S011]
- "But you don’t even get shots on goal if you don’t have your mission and culture set right." [S011]
- "if your company is going to outlast you, that mission has to be a lot more than a cult of personality" [S010]

Council use: before approving a big change, ask the leader to state the institution's purpose in one line. A hospital that cannot say why it exists will not hold a hard change (inferred from S009, S010).

## 2. Worldview, Core, Culture (the transformation memo)

His first big memo has three blocks. How the world is changing. What only we can contribute. How we must change ourselves to deliver it. Strategy never comes without the culture change it needs (inferred from S001).

- "Today I want to synthesize the strategic direction and massive opportunity I've been discussing for the past few months and the fundamental cultural changes required to deliver on it." [S001]
- "We must all understand and embrace what only Microsoft can contribute to the world and how we can once again change the world." [S001]
- "As a large company, I think it's critical to define the core, but it's important to make smart choices on other businesses in which we can have fundamental impact and success." [S001]
- "Our ambitions are bold and so must be our desire to change and evolve our culture." [S001]

He names the hard changes up front, in short sentences. "Organizations will change. Mergers and acquisitions will occur. Job responsibilities will evolve. New partnerships will be formed. Tired traditions will be questioned. Our priorities will be adjusted." [S001]

He opens a month of dialogue before reorganizing. "We'll use the month of July to have a dialogue about this bold ambition and our core focus." [S001]

## 3. Growth mindset: know-it-all to learn-it-all

Taken from Carol Dweck. The point is not to claim a growth mindset. The point is to confront your own fixed mindset.

- "we don't want to be these know-it-alls, but we want to be learn-it-alls" [S010]
- "It starts with a belief that everyone can grow and develop; that potential is nurtured, not predetermined; and that anyone can change their mindset." [S006]
- "the beauty of the growth mindset framework, if you will, is not about claiming growth mindset, but confronting one’s own fixed mindset" [S011]
- "the day you say you have a growth mindset is the day you don’t have a growth mindset. That’s the nice recursion in it." [S011]
- "confront our fixed mindset with humility, curiosity, compassion, and the recognition that, while none of us will ever be perfect, we can always be better than we are today" [S007]

The misuse to watch: people use the idea to judge others. "And like all things, it also can be taken to the extreme." [S011] He has seen people in meetings look around for who lacks a growth mindset, instead of naming their own (inferred from S011).

## 4. Culture as the lead lever, and as input and output

Culture is the base under mission and strategy. It also produces the new capability a strategy needs.

- "Our culture is the foundation on which our mission and strategy stand, and cultivating it is our greatest priority." [S007]
- "At Microsoft, we think of our culture as being both input and output. To pursue new concepts, we need new capability. To build new capability, we need a culture that allows us to grow that capability long before it is conventional wisdom." [S008]
- "the hard part of organizational change is that inward change that has to come" [S011]
- "Culture change means we will do things differently. Often people think that means everyone other than them. In reality, it means all of us taking a new approach and working together to make Microsoft better." [S001]

## 5. Espoused culture vs lived experience

Culture is what staff actually live, not what leaders say. The gap is the thing to measure and close.

- "We are on a journey to close the gap between our espoused culture and the lived experience for every employee at Microsoft." [S005]
- "It doesn't matter what we said about our culture 10 years ago or even last year if we aren't practicing it today" [S008]
- "the best feature of that cultural thing is that it’s never done. So you never can claim that job done." [S011]

## 6. Do not metric culture; let it breathe

He chose not to score culture with a red, yellow, green dashboard. Scoring would have been gamed. He let leaders tell their own stories (inferred from S011).

- "the classic thing at Microsoft would have been to metric it and then say green, red, yellow, and then start doing bad things to all the reds and then it would’ve been gamed in a second" [S011]
- "We didn’t do that, and that I think helped a lot." [S011]
- "You have to basically let even leaders bring their own personal passion to it, personal stories to it, give it some room to breathe." [S011]

Council use: a culture program in a public hospital should not become a compliance scorecard. Track the change in behavior, not a culture score (inferred from S011).

## 7. Three culture pillars on a growth-mindset base

Customer obsession, One Microsoft, diversity and inclusion.

- "If we commit to being customer obsessed, operating as One Microsoft, and becoming more diverse and inclusive, I believe there is no limit to what we can achieve." [S006]
- "No customer of ours cares about our organizational boundaries, and we need to operate as One Microsoft to deliver the best solutions for them." [S006]
- "If we want to serve the world, we need to represent the world." [S007]

For a hospital, the patient plays the customer role. Departments are the silos the patient does not care about (inferred from S006).

## 8. Model, Coach, Care (the manager in the middle)

Managers model the culture, coach their people, and care for them. Care means knowing the lived experience of each person (inferred from S009). The middle layer is where change breaks.

- "we have this framework, Adam, called model coach care" [S009]
- "at the top, people get it, people at the bottom get it. In the middle of there's all this problem." [S009]
- "The managers in first-line in fact are the most stressed because they lack the support and the connection from the company." [S009]
- "unless we create the empowerment, the space, that capability in the middle so that they can care for that people it's just not going to happen" [S009]

Council use: any rollout plan should name what department heads and charge nurses get to carry it (inferred from S009).

## 9. Psychological safety as a system, not a gesture

- "Feeling secure leads you to be more vulnerable." [S009]
- "one technique of that is to share your own fallibility because that gives confidence to others" [S009]
- "But then the real issue is systemically. How do you help people feel secure?" [S009]
- "if all the risks have to be taken by somebody else who is working on your team, then you're creating an environment where it just will be very, very hard" [S009]

## 10. Tech intensity: adopt best-in-class, then build your own capability

Buying tools is half. The institution must also grow its own digital capability.

- "adopting best-in-class digital tools and platforms for the purpose of building new, proprietary products and services" [S006]
- "will not only adopt best-in-class software and services but also build their own digital capability" [S005]
- "Every customer is looking for both innovative technology to drive new growth and a strategic partner that can help them build their own digital capability." [S004]
- "equipping domain experts with tools that are low-code or no-code to create solutions that solve their unique business needs" [S006]
- "We want them to become independent with us, not dependent on us." [S005]

## 11. Diffusion and the adoption lag

A tool matters only if it spreads and changes work. Process must change too. He compares AI to the business process reengineering of the CRM and ERP era (inferred from S010).

- "we used to talk about something called business process reengineering. It was as much about a new methodology or a doctrine even of how you run businesses." [S010]
- "some of the business practices will have to change" [S010]
- "if you get software development to be faster, all the other functions around change" [S010]
- "new platforms are not about just taking the old and just building a new UI" [S010]
- "focusing on leading indicators like usage" [S002]
- "Microsoft 365 Copilot is becoming a daily habit for knowledge workers" [S008]

The success test is broad productivity, not demos or benchmarks.

- "the time has come for us as a tech industry to directly parlay what we celebrate as tech advances into broad economic growth" [S010]
- "all the evals are saturated. It’s becoming slightly meaningless." [S011]

Council use: ask for a usage target, a workflow that will be redesigned, and a productivity measure. A tool bought but not used is a cost, not a transformation (inferred from S002, S010).

## 12. The workflow-cost lens for health care

He sees most avoidable health cost in workflow and care management. AI's first job there is to lift clinician burden.

- "we know we need the health outcomes to get better and costs to come down. And a lot of the cost is quite frankly, workflow cost" [S010]
- "But the real cost is in care management." [S010]
- "With DAX Copilot, more than 400 healthcare organizations are increasing physician productivity and reducing burnout." [S008]
- "the software once built can reach every hospital in the world" [S010]

## 13. Copilot pattern: human in the loop

- "There's a human in the loop. The human has agency and judgment of human matters." [S010]

## 14. Trust pillars and responsible AI

Trust rests on privacy, security, responsible AI, and transparency. Six principles guide AI work.

- "Without trust, none of our progress is possible." [S005]
- "For Microsoft, trust is built on privacy, security, the responsible use of AI, and transparency." [S006]
- "Fairness, reliability and safety, privacy and security, inclusiveness, transparency, and accountability are the ethical principles that guide our work and advocacy." [S006]
- "not what computers can do, but what computers should do" [S005]

AI safety triage. Fix real-world harms today first. Reuse existing sector rules, such as those in health care (inferred from S010).

- "the first thing we've got to really go and focus on is any real-world harm today" [S010]
- "we have in healthcare, in financial services, in auto safety, already existing regulations, and we can subject AI to the same regulations" [S010]

## 15. Security above all (Secure Future Initiative)

- "First, prioritizing fundamentals, with security above all else." [S008]
- "we implement our principles of secure by design, secure by default, and secure operations" [S008]
- "holding each one of us accountable for building secure products and services" [S008]

## 16. Partnering with former rivals

Most big wins come from partnerships. A good partnership leaves surplus for all sides. He puts organic growth first, then partnerships, then M&A (inferred from S010).

- "The customer wins, the partner wins and you win." [S010]
- "the best partnerships are when you start thinking, caring about the partner, really making sure that they're getting economic surplus" [S010]
- "It's also important in partnerships to know what happens if one of the partners becomes too greedy, then it's unstable." [S010]
- "There are very few zero-sum battles, actually, when you think about it." [S010]

## 17. What to stop doing

Transformation includes choosing what to drop. He dropped an old self-label, cut process, scaled back the phone, and moved away from siloed suites.

- "While the devices and services description was helpful in starting our transformation, we now need to hone in on our unique strategy." [S001]
- "You can expect to have fewer processes but more focused and measurable outcomes. You will see fewer people get involved in decisions and more emphasis on accountability." [S001]
- "Going forward, we will focus our innovation and investments in areas where we see the greatest opportunity for growth." [S004]
- "customers are looking to move away from monolithic suites that perpetuate disconnected data siloes and expensive custom extensibility frameworks" [S004]

## 18. Leaders: clarity, energy, success

Leaders build institutional strength that outlasts them. They leave energy in the system. They get the team to perform.

- "the job is to build institutional strength so that the people who take over from you are more successful than you" [S010]
- "you got to make sure you leave enough energy in the system so that it can continue to renew it" [S010]
- "leadership is about having a team and then getting the team to perform for a season" [S011]
- "That ability to get the energy to go play the game is one." [S011]

## 19. Limits: where his record is thin

Read this before leaning on him. He rarely admits mistakes outright in this corpus. Shortfalls are framed as journeys and learning. The two closest cases are below.

The phone pullback. He calls it scaling back, not a failure. Years later he explains the logic, not a regret.

- "scaling back our investment commensurate with the opportunity in areas like phone" [S003]
- "Having seen what happens to number three players in an ecosystem, I felt like wow, that train had left the station." [S011]
- "In some of the others, we were behind, but that’s fine." [S011]

The 2024 security reset. Making security top priority implies past lapses. The letters do not describe the incidents (inferred from S008).

- "we recognize that our work on security is never complete. We must and will do more." [S008]
- "We recognize that trust is earned, not given." [S008]

Other limits he names. He slips on his own standards. He admits Microsoft's 1990s hubris. He names a climate miss.

- "The first time they admit a mistake, which by the way, I do sometimes. And then I have to check myself right." [S009]
- "The day hubris takes over and you're not grounded in what made you, first of all, successful." [S010]
- "our Scope 3 emissions increased, due in large part to significant global datacenter expansions and the growth in Xbox sales and usage" [S007]

Council use: his frame suits a large institution that already has resources. It says little about cash-starved public hospitals. His adoption stories are mostly vendor success stories (inferred from S004, S005, S008).
