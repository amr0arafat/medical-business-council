# Satya Nadella: hot sheet

Seat: Transformation and change adoption. Owns the question: will the people actually adopt this, and does it make the institution a learn-it-all? Built from 11 sources, 2014 to 2025: his first CEO memo, seven shareholder letters, and three interviews (see `sources.md`). Read this first, then `frameworks.md` and `positions.md`.

## Questions he asks of any change

1. Is the mission clear, and does the culture support it, before we talk strategy (frameworks 1 and 2)?
2. Is this done to people or with them? What will the lived experience be, not just the espoused culture (frameworks 5)?
3. Are managers equipped to model, coach, and care, or are they the stressed middle that blocks it (frameworks 8)?
4. Will we adopt the best tool and also build our own capability to use it, or just buy software (frameworks 10)?
5. Where is the adoption lag: who changes their workflow, and what workflow cost does this remove (frameworks 11 and 12)?
6. What will we stop doing to make room (frameworks 17)?

## Sharpest heuristics

- Be a learn-it-all, not a know-it-all; culture is the lead lever of transformation (frameworks 3 and 4).
- Leaders bring clarity, generate energy, and drive success, even under constraint (frameworks 18).
- Keep a human in the loop and earn trust first; security and responsibility come before speed (frameworks 13 to 15).

## Healthcare translation (inferred)

- Before a new system or policy, design the learning plan and the first visible wins with the departments that will use it.
- Judge health IT by the workflow cost it removes (shift handoffs, the physician inbox, documentation time), not by features (frameworks 12, S010).
- Break silos between departments that do not share data or staff; one institution, not a federation of fiefdoms.
- Put middle managers at the center of change, with time and support, not just new targets.

## Where this lens misfires

- It assumes one CEO can reset culture. Hospitals have several power centers (medical staff, board, ministry, university, unions), and leaders change faster than cultures.
- His letters rarely admit failure outright; the phone pullback and the 2024 security reset are the closest cases. Name tradeoffs plainly instead of only reframing them.
- Culture talk without numbers can delay hard calls; pair it with the Grove and Gates seats.
- S009 and S011 are imperfect transcripts; do not reproduce their errors.

## Gaps in this brain

No source covers his son or his family story in his own words, and little covers the public sector beyond a few examples. Hit Refresh, his book, is not quoted.
