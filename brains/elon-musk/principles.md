# Elon Musk: principles

Short rules he states directly. One line each: the rule, then his words. S009 to S011 are third-party transcriptions; S012 and S013 are articles quoting him.

## Requirements and deletion

- Question every requirement, whoever wrote it. "The requirements are definitely dumb; it does not matter who gave them to you." [S011]
- Smart authors make requirements more dangerous, not less. "It's particularly dangerous if a smart person gave you the requirements. You might not question them enough." [S010]
- Delete before you improve. "Then, try very hard to delete the part or process. This is actually very important." [S010]
- If nothing comes back, you did not cut enough. "If you're not patiently adding things back in, you're not deleting enough." [S010]
- Distrust the in-case argument. "But you can basically make 'in case' arguments for so many things." [S010]
- Do not optimize what should not exist. "the most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize something that should simply not exist" [S011]
- Speed comes after the first three steps. "But don't go faster until you've worked on the other three things first." [S010]
- Automate last. "And then the final step is automate." [S010]

## Thinking

- Reason from fundamentals for new problems. "But when you want to do something new, you have to apply the physics approach." [S005]
- Physics is a framework for thinking. "I do think there's a good framework for thinking. It is physics." [S005]
- Reality settles arguments. "Reality is the ultimate judge." [S007]
- Minimize confident errors. "you don't want to be confidently wrong, so you're not going to be right every time, but you want to minimize how often you're confidently wrong" [S007]
- Go to the primary source. "What did the judge actually write? What does the jury and the judge actually conclude?" [S007]
- Look for the real cause, not the claimed one. "trying to figure out what really was the cause of victory in any particular case as opposed to what one side or another claim the reason" [S007]
- Seek negative feedback. "really pay attention to negative feedback, and solicit it, particularly from friends." [S005]
- Think in probabilities. "I look at the future from the standpoint of probabilities." [S006]
- Agreement between past opponents is a truth signal. "It makes sense that if people who in the past have disagreed, agree about something, it's probably true." [S007]

## Cost and constraints

- Find the one problem that dwarfs the rest. "This problem of affordability dwarfs all others." [S003]
- Check costs before you start building. "So it is critical that we thoroughly examine the probable cost of alternatives to replacing the Shuttle before embarking upon a new development." [S003]
- Hard problems get solved; time is the variable. "It will get solved. It's the question of how long it takes to solve it." [S007]
- Scarcity forces efficiency. "Tesla's approach is fundamentally more compute efficient. It had to be, there was no choice." [S007]
- Volume drives cost down. "Without economies of scale, anything we built would be expensive, whether it was an economy sedan or a sports car." [S002]
- Reuse only pays if it is fast and complete. "reusability is only relevant if it is rapid and complete." [S006]
- Progress takes work. "It only improves if a lot of people work very hard to make it better" [S006]
- Cost cutting is mostly effort, not genius. "It's mostly just a lot of hard work." [S008]
- Cut what nobody uses first. "if no one's using the software, we can terminate this software license agreement" [S008]

## Meetings and communication (2018 Tesla email, quoted by CNBC)

- Keep large meetings rare and short. Stay only "unless you're certain they are providing value to the whole audience, in which case keep them very short" [S013]
- Frequent meetings only for urgent matters. "Meeting frequency should drop rapidly once the urgent matter is resolved." [S013]
- No jargon. "Don't use acronyms or nonsense words for objects, software or processes at Tesla." [S013]
- Anything needing an explanation slows people down. "In general, anything that requires an explanation inhibits communication." [S013]
- Let information cross levels. "A major source of issues is poor communication between depts. The way to solve this is allow free flow of information between all levels." [S013]
- Talk directly and act. "It must be ok for people to talk directly and just make the right thing happen." [S013]
- Enforcing chain of command is a firing offense. "Any manager who attempts to enforce chain of command communication will soon find themselves working elsewhere." [S013]
- Common sense beats rules. "In general, always pick common sense as your guide." [S013]

## Organization and urgency

- Remove walls between units that serve one customer. "One ordering experience, one installation, one service contact, one phone app." [S002]
- Urgency raises the odds; it does not guarantee them. "It's still just a chance, not for sure." [S012]
- One good example opens a sector. "I think it's really critical to have that one good example." [S004]
- Customers judge fairly. "The consumer is an incredibly objective judge of product." [S004]

## Government, procurement and regulation

- Competition is the forcing function. "So we've really seen no truly new entrants to the American launch-vehicle market, and, therefore, should not be surprised that costs remain unchanged." [S004]
- Prizes are the top lever for lawmakers. "If I can emphasize, underscore, and highlight one strategy for Congress, it is to offer prizes of meaningful scale and scope." [S003]
- An unwon prize costs nothing. "If a prize is not won, it costs us nothing." [S003]
- Prizes remove evaluator bias. "The result is an efficient Darwinian exercise with the subjectivity and error of proposal evaluation removed." [S003]
- No major award without competition. "it should never have been awarded without full competition." [S003]
- Ask for a fair process, not a win. "SpaceX is only asking for a fair shot to meet the objectives, not demanding to win the contract." [S003]
- Government should buy services, not compete. "That requires full and unfettered access in which the government buys services, and does not enter into competition with us, using the public's money." [S004]
- Minimum burden for reasonable public safety is the test. "I believe that should be the key criterion." [S004]
- Blanket approvals save time and money. "This would save a substantial amount of capital and processing time." [S004]
- Rewrite rulebooks from zero, for simplicity. "we suggest funding a zero-based revision of EWR-127-1 and the FAA equivalent documentation with a focus on simplicity" [S004]
- Spending nobody can explain should stop (2025-05-30). "nobody even knows why the money's being spent. It's truly absurd." [S008]
- Big savings take time (2025-05-30). "We can't do it in a few months." [S008]

## People

- Judge people by track record. "I guess you look at somebody's track record over time, and I guess you use your neural net to assess someone." [S007]
- Do not take a cynic's view as neutral. "Never trust a cynic." [S007]
- Drop grudges. "holding onto grudges does not affect the future in a good way" [S007]
- Everyone errs. "All designs are wrong, it's just a matter of how wrong." [S011]
