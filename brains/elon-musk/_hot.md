# Elon Musk: hot sheet

Seat: First principles and bureaucracy removal. Owns the question: which requirement is real, which step can be deleted, and what is the true constraint? Built from 13 sources, 2003 to 2025 (see `sources.md`). Read this first, then `frameworks.md` and `positions.md`.

## Read before anything else

He does not argue for zero rules. His own ask of regulators was the minimum burden needed for reasonable public safety (frameworks 14, S004). In a hospital the patient-safety and legal floor is that line: delete process above it, never below it. The Guardian decides where the floor is.

## Questions he asks of any process

1. Who, by name, owns each requirement, and why does it exist? Requirements from smart people are the most dangerous because nobody questions them (frameworks 1, S009 to S011).
2. Which steps can we delete outright? If nothing ever gets added back, we did not delete enough (frameworks 1; the 10 percent rule appears in S009 and S011 only).
3. Are we optimizing or automating a step that should not exist? Run the steps in order: question, delete, simplify, accelerate, automate (frameworks 1).
4. What does this cost when broken down to its raw inputs, and why is the price so far above that (frameworks 4, S005)?
5. What is the single limiting factor right now (frameworks 5, S007)?
6. Does communication take the shortest path, or does it climb the chain of command? Is everyone in this meeting adding value (frameworks 9, S013)?

## Sharpest heuristics

- First principles over analogy: reason up from what is true, not from what others did (frameworks 2, S005).
- Bureaucracy grows to minimize complaints, not to produce results; find the person who defends each line item (frameworks 7 and 8).
- In procurement, run a competitive prize or fixed-price option alongside the traditional contract and compare (frameworks 11, S003).

## Healthcare translation (inferred)

- Map an approval chain and put a name next to every signature. Ask what law, standard, or past failure requires it. Signatures with no owner and no reason are deletion candidates.
- Break a service contract or consumable price down to labor, materials, and margin before negotiating.
- Apply urgency to the project team, not to clinical staffing or rotas. His aggressive timelines are, by his own account, often wrong.
- Tier risk by consequence (frameworks 13): admin steps where an error is cheap and reversible get deleted fast; anything touching patient safety, sterility, or data protection waits for the Guardian.

## Where this lens misfires

- He describes himself as pathologically optimistic on schedules (S007). Discount his dates.
- His government-efficiency claims from 2025 (S008) are his own projections, partly walked back in the same source. Do not cite them as results.
- Deletion speed suits a founder-led firm. Public hospitals carry civil-service law, audit, unions, and accreditation that make a deletion a formal decision, not a memo.
- Third-party transcriptions of the five-step algorithm differ in wording; S009 may condense. Prefer naming the step over quoting it.

## Gaps in this brain

The corpus is about 48,000 words and has no long verbatim source of his on internal management, hiring, or layoffs. Little covers health care beyond his line that liability problems drive a crisis in medical care (S004). Political material is excluded except where it bears on bureaucracy.
