# Elon Musk: stories

Anecdotes he tells, kept close to his words. Each ends with when to use it on a hospital or research-center decision. S009 to S011 transcribe the same 2021 Starbase talk in slightly different words. S012 and S013 are articles.

## 1. The Model 3 line: automating what should have been deleted (2021)

On the Starbase tour he described the Model 3 battery-pack line, where he ran his own algorithm in reverse (inferred from S009).
"Multiple times I automated, accelerated, simplified, and then deleted." [S010]
"It was like being in a Dilbert cartoon." [S009]
The lesson he draws: "The reason this is the third step and not the first step is because the most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize something that should simply not exist" [S011]
"Because if you are digging your grave, you don't want to dig faster. You want to stop digging your grave." [S011]
Context from 2018 (writer's account, S013): Tesla paused production for several days to upgrade equipment while chasing 6,000 Model 3 cars a week, and he was sleeping at the factory.

Use it when: a department asks to buy automation, a new IT system, or a robot for a workflow nobody has first tried to shorten or delete.

## 2. The fish license (2023)

The FAA had approved a Starship launch, but the launch waited on Fish and Wildlife (inferred from S007).
"We effectively need a fish license to launch a rocket." [S007]
"This is all real. This is actually what happened." [S007]

Use it when: a project with one approval in hand is stalled by a second, unrelated sign-off, and you need to find who owns that step and whether it applies.

## 3. The shark probability (2023)

Regulators worried a rocket stage might hit a shark. They would not share shark data, citing fin hunters. Another part of the same agency could run the analysis but was not trusted with the data (inferred from S007).
"As a percentage of ocean surface area, sharks basically are zero." [S007]
"They're literally in your department." [S007]
"Eventually they managed to solve the internal quandary, and indeed the probability of us hitting a shark is essentially zero." [S007]

Use it when: two units of the same hospital or ministry block each other over data access, and a simple base-rate estimate would settle the question.

## 4. The seals of Vandenberg (2003 and 2023)

In 2003 he told Congress the seal population near Vandenberg had grown 12.7 percent, yet SpaceX still paid $10,000 per launch to monitor them (inferred from S004).
"certain issues of environmental concern seem to defy common sense." [S004]
"it seems clear that if anything the Vandenberg launch activity serves as an aphrodisiac." [S004]
Twenty years later the requirement was still there. The concern was that sonic booms would disturb seal procreation, though "the seal population has steadily increased" [S007]
"Nonetheless, we were forced to kidnap a seal, strap it to a board, put headphones on the seal and play sonic boom sounds to it to see if it would be distressed." [S007]
"Twice, by the way, we had to do it twice." [S007]

Use it when: a recurring audit, monitoring task, or compliance study keeps running even though years of data have already answered its question.

## 5. Millions of unused software licenses (2025)

At the DOGE farewell press conference (2025-05-30) he described what line-item review found.
"we find situations where there are millions of software licenses where with zero people using them, zero, exactly." [S008]
He framed the cause as structural: "it's the banal evil of bureaucracy. It's sort of the, frankly, largely, largely uncaring nature of bureaucracy." [S008]
He cited a maxim he attributed to Tom Friedman: "money is spent most poorly when it is someone else's money being spent on people you don't know" [S008]
Recipients will not flag waste themselves: "you're not going to hear someone confessing that they receive money inappropriately. Never." [S008]
The savings totals he gave at the same event are his projections, not verified results.

Use it when: starting a cost review in a public hospital. Begin with licenses, contracts, and assets that have zero users.

## 6. The DOGE boogeyman (2025)

A friend's daughter at Georgetown law believed DOGE had cut Senate legal internships. He says DOGE had nothing to do with it (inferred from S008).
"we became essentially the DOGE boogeyman where if any cut anywhere would be ascribed to DOGE" [S008]

Use it when: setting up an efficiency unit or committee. Expect every unpopular cut to be blamed on it, and publish what it did and did not decide.

## 7. The Shuttle: reusable on paper (2004, 2013)

"The Shuttle today costs about a factor of ten more per flight than originally projected." [S003]
"the parts that were reusable took a 10,000-person group nine months to refurbish for flight." [S005]
"So the space shuttle ended up costing a billion dollars per flight." [S005]
Writer's account (S012): he said the Shuttle's design froze because every mission was crewed, so any change was high risk and low reward.

Use it when: a vendor sells reusable or refurbishable equipment. Ask for the full turnaround cost and time, not the sticker price.

## 8. Early SpaceX: cheap launch on one founder's money (2002 to 2008)

"I was trying to figure out the fastest way to turn a large fortune into a small one." [S005]
"I thought our chances of success were so low that I didn't want to risk anyone's funds in the beginning but my own." [S002]
In 2003 he priced Falcon at $6M a flight. "This is a reduction of over 75 percent, compared with our nearest incumbent competitor." [S004]
He began with satellites: "The reasoning for an early focus on satellites is that we feel this is the path of least market risk" [S004]
"We came very close to failure, but we managed to get through that point in 2008." [S005]

Use it when: launching a new service or research core. Start with the lowest-risk customer segment.

## 9. Government launch programs and the sole-source award (2003, 2004)

"In fact, they have failed even to reach the launch pad, let alone get to space." [S003]
In 2004 he protested a NASA award to Kistler made without competition. He said Kistler had been bankrupt since the prior July and planned to launch from Australia with Russian engines (inferred from S003).
"the exclusive bias, really, of NASA, as far as contracting for any significant project, is to go to the incumbent corporations" [S004]
Unfair awards hurt the whole market, "not just on the particular company treated unfairly, but on all private capital considering entering the space launch business." [S003]
His alternative was the prize. "vastly more than the $10 million prize is being spent by the dozens of teams that hope to win." [S003]
"If a prize is not won, it costs us nothing." [S003]

Use it when: a tender is drifting toward the usual incumbent supplier, or when a small outcome-based prize could test cheaper bidders alongside a large contract.

## 10. General aviation and liability (2003)

"General aviation almost perished in the 1980's as a result of one massive jury award of questionable justice after another." [S004]
"aviation was a strong and mature industry with a well developed immune system." [S004]
He linked this to health care in his prepared statement (see positions, S004).

Use it when: weighing a new clinical or research service whose main barrier is open-ended liability, and the question is whether informed consent and caps make it viable.

## 11. The tunnel that is twice too wide (2017)

"So it's roughly a billion dollars a mile to do the subway extension in LA." [S006]
"a single road lane tunnel according to regulations has to be 26 feet, maybe 28 feet in diameter" [S006]
That size serves crashes, emergency vehicles and exhaust ventilation. His electric skates needed about 12 feet (inferred from S006).
"the tunneling cost scales with the cross-sectional area." [S006]
His benchmark was a snail named Gary. "Victory is beating the snail." [S006]

Use it when: a building code or design standard written for one use is being applied to a different, lower-risk use, such as a clinic or lab fit-out.

## 12. The relay chain (2018 email)

He described a message passing from worker to manager to director to vice president and back down, ending "who talks to a manager, who talks to someone doing the actual work, then super dumb things will happen" [S013]
"It must be ok for people to talk directly and just make the right thing happen." [S013]

Use it when: a ward problem needs a pharmacy or IT fix and staff are told to go through department heads first.

## 13. No motor at any price (2023)

For the Optimus robot, "None of them were suitable for a humanoid robot, literally none. So, we had to develop our own." [S007]

Use it when: deciding whether to build an in-house tool or service because no vendor product fits the real requirement.
