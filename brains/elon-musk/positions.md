# Elon Musk: positions

Pillar: First principles and bureaucracy removal. Core question: Which requirement is real, which step can be deleted, and what is the true constraint?

Dates follow the source list. S003 and S004 are congressional testimony (2003, 2004). S009 to S011 are third-party transcriptions of one Starbase tour talk (July 2021) and differ in wording. S012 and S013 are articles; only words they quote from him count as his. His DOGE figures in S008 are his own projections, not verified results.

## 1. Process and management

**The five-step algorithm (latest full statement, 2021).** The order matters.
Step 1, question the requirement. "Just make your requirements less dumb." [S010]
"The requirements are definitely dumb; it does not matter who gave them to you." [S011]
Each requirement needs an owner. "each required part and process must come from a name, not a department, as a department cannot be asked why a requirement exists, but a person can" [S009] (S009 may condense his words; S012 gives the same idea as paraphrase.)
Step 2, delete. "Then, try very hard to delete the part or process. This is actually very important." [S010]
"If you're not patiently adding things back in, you're not deleting enough." [S010]
His benchmark is adding back at least 10% of what you delete. "If parts are not being added back into the design at least 10% of the time" [S011]
Step 3, simplify. "The most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize a thing that should not exist" [S010]
Step 4, speed up. "The fourth step is to accelerate cycle time. You're moving too slowly. Go faster. But don't go faster until you've worked on the other three things first." [S010]
"If you're digging your grave, don't dig it faster." [S009]
Step 5, automate last. "And then the final step is automate." [S010]
Remove checks once a process is proven. "An important part of this is to remove in-process testing after the problems have been diagnosed" [S009] (lower-confidence verbatim).
Self-correction: he says he ran the steps backward. "I have personally made the mistake of going backward on all five steps multiple times." [S011]

**First principles over analogy (2013, restated 2023).** "boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy." [S005]
"But when you want to do something new, you have to apply the physics approach." [S005]
2023 version: "For something important you need to reason with from first principles and think about things in the limit one direction or the other." [S007]
"You have to test any conclusions against the ground truth of reality. Reality is the ultimate judge." [S007]

**Meetings (2018 email).** "Excessive meetings are the blight of big companies and almost always get worse over time." [S013]
"Meeting frequency should drop rapidly once the urgent matter is resolved." [S013]
"Walk out of a meeting or drop off a call as soon as it is obvious you aren't adding value. It is not rude to leave, it is rude to make someone stay and waste their time." [S013]

**Communication skips the chain of command (2018 email).** "Communication should travel via the shortest path necessary to get the job done, not through the 'chain of command'." [S013]
"Any manager who attempts to enforce chain of command communication will soon find themselves working elsewhere." [S013]

**Change silly rules, do not just ignore them (2018 email).** "In general, always pick common sense as your guide." [S013]
"If following a 'company rule' is obviously ridiculous in a particular situation, such that it would make for a great Dilbert cartoon, then the rule should change." [S013]

**Feedback and error (2013, 2023).** "really pay attention to negative feedback, and solicit it, particularly from friends." [S005]
"you don't want to be confidently wrong, so you're not going to be right every time, but you want to minimize how often you're confidently wrong" [S007]

**Break down organisational walls (2016).** "We can't do this well if Tesla and SolarCity are different companies, which is why we need to combine and break down the barriers inherent to being separate companies." [S002]

## 2. Cost and manufacturing

**Find the one real constraint (2004, 2023).** "I do not think there are multiple problems in space; I think there is one, and that is the cost of access to space." [S003]
In 2023 the frame is a moving bottleneck. "So at various points, there's some kind of limiting factor to progress and with regard to AI, I'm saying right now the limiting factor is silicon chips" [S007]
He predicts the next one: "The initial constraint being literally voltage step down transformers" [S007]

**Break cost into its elements (2003, 2013, 2017).** "Our approach has been to focus on reducing all the cost elements of a launch vehicle company, those being propulsion, structures, avionics, launch operations and general overhead." [S004]
Reuse is the lever: "the cost of the propellant is about .3 percent of the cost of the rocket." [S005]
"reusability is only relevant if it is rapid and complete." [S006]
Stack small gains: "Combine that and that's a factor of eight." [S006]

**Manufacturing over design (2016, latest 2021).** "Tesla engineering has transitioned to focus heavily on designing the machine that makes the machine" [S002]
Writer's account (S012, 2021): he says manufacturing is underrated and design overrated, and a production system is 10 to 100 times harder than designing the product.

**Enter high, then drive down cost (2006, 2013).** "Almost any new technology initially has high unit cost before it can be optimized and this is no less true for electric cars." [S001]

**Make it work, then make it efficient (2023).** "In general, with any given technology, you first try to make it work, and then you make it efficient." [S007]

**Build it yourself when the market fails you (2023).** When no supplier could provide suitable robot motors, Tesla designed its own (inferred from S007).

## 3. Regulation and government

**Government as customer, not rival (2003).** "We seek government as a customer, not a competitor." [S004]
He accepts that some work is the state's job. "it's difficult to generate a commercial return from such things. So that is, sort of, necessarily the funding purview of government" [S004]

**Why big programs fail (2003, 2004).** "One multibillion dollar government program after another has failed." [S003]
"Unlike standard contracting, where failure is often perversely rewarded with more money, failure to win a prize costs us nothing." [S003]
"If you shut down the process of new companies getting into such a market, you won't see the price decrease, you won't see the quality improvement, because there is no forcing function for incumbents to change." [S004]

**Prizes in parallel with contracts (2004).** "If I can emphasize, underscore, and highlight one strategy for Congress, it is to offer prizes of meaningful scale and scope." [S003]
"parallel every major NASA contract award with a prize valued at one tenth of the contract amount." [S003]
"If you get an estimate, whatever the NASA estimate is to get something done, erase a zero and make that a prize." [S003]

**Fair procurement (2004).** "It is critical that the Government acts and is perceived to act fairly in its award of contracts." [S003]
On a sole-source award: "it should never have been awarded without full competition." [S003]
He allowed he might be wrong. "it is possible that NASA has made the right decision in this case." [S003]

**Minimum regulatory burden (2003).** "providing only the minimum regulatory burden necessary to ensure reasonable safety for the general public." [S004]
Judge regulators by progress enabled: "progress in fostering new launch vehicle developments should be a key metric of success in the performance evaluation of FAA-AST and other federal agencies" [S004]
Blanket approval for routine work: "a categorical exclusion, such as exists for airplanes, would be immensely helpful." [S004]
Rewrite from zero rather than amend: "the right approach is to do a ground up revision." [S004]

**Regulatory load as the binding constraint (2023).** "We're subject to, I think over a hundred regulatory agencies domestically and internationally. It's a lot." [S007]
"the limiting factor for SpaceX for Starship launch is regulatory approval" [S007]

**Supports AI oversight (2023).** "I've been pushing for some kind of regulatory oversight for a long time." [S007]
"I think it would be wise for us to have at least an objective third party who can be like a referee" [S007]
Not a contradiction in his framing: he wants AI oversight while objecting to how some agency reviews are run (inferred from S007).

**Bureaucracy and DOGE (2025-05-30, his last stated view).** He served as a special government employee at DOGE and left in May 2025.
Diagnosis: "you can't really even blame the individuals because the way the government works is complaint minimization." [S008]
"their manager will say, it's not worth the trouble, just pay it anyway. That happens over and over again." [S008]
Method: "It's going through really millions of line items and saying just each one of them makes sense or not make sense." [S008]
"So for a lot of the expenses, there is actually no defender at all." [S008]
Figures, his projections only, not verified in the source: "The calculations of the DOGE team thus far in terms of an FY 25 to FY 26 delta are over 160 billion." [S008]
"I'm confident that over time we'll see a trillion dollars of savings and reduction, a trillion dollars of waste, of fraud reduction." [S008]
The same answers also mention "$8 trillion in waste and fraud reductions" [S008]. The source does not reconcile the two figures.
He conceded some cuts blamed on DOGE were bad: "including things that made no sense and we would agree, made no sense" [S008]
"We can't do it in a few months." [S008]

## 4. Safety and liability

**Safety is probabilistic (2016, 2017).** "The thing to appreciate about vehicle safety is this is probabilistic." [S006]
"So really the key threshold for autonomy is how much better does autonomy need to be than a person before you can rely on it?" [S006]

**Delay can be the unsafe choice (2016).** "it would therefore be morally reprehensible to delay release simply for fear of bad press or some mercantile calculation of legal liability." [S002]

**Tier risk by consequence (2021, writer's account, S012).** Crewed Dragon can never fail and gets extreme testing. Uncrewed Starship can iterate and fail often. "Starship does not have anyone on board so we can blow things up. It's really helpful." [S012]

**Liability caps (2003).** "One of the surest ways of preventing companies like SpaceX from offering human transportation in the future is to make it an unlimited liability business" [S004]
The cap would not cover "clearly egregious conduct" [S004]
"an individual should have the right to waive liability provided the risks are fully explained" [S004]
He drew the medical link himself: "We are seeing a crisis in medical care for similar reasons." [S004]

## 5. Timelines and risk

**Self-correction on schedules (latest, 2023).** "I would say that I'm pathologically optimistic on schedule. This is true." [S007]
"I've been far too optimistic about autopilot." [S007]
Earlier dated forecast, 2017: "we should be able to go all the way from a parking lot in California to a parking lot in New York, no controls touched at any point during the entire journey." [S006] It was framed as a target: "Aspirationally, that's our target. Our internal targets are more aggressive" [S006]

**Software takes longer than hardware (2016).** "refinement and validation of the software will take much longer than putting in place the cameras, radar, sonar and computing hardware." [S002]

**Simulate what you cannot test (2023).** "All this has got to be simulated because you don't get very many kicks at the can." [S007]

## 6. Technology and AI

**Care on AI (2023).** "I think we want to be very careful in how we develop AI." [S007]
"It unfortunately hallucinates most when you at least want it to hallucinate." [S007]
