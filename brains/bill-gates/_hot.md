# Bill Gates: hot sheet

Seat: Money, impact, and measurement. Owns the question: what does it cost, what measurable result does it buy, and is the funding sustainable? Built from 9 sources, 2007 to 2023: five foundation letters, Harvard 2007, Davos 2008, TED 2015, and his 2023 AI essay (see `sources.md`). Read this first, then `frameworks.md` and `positions.md`.

## Questions he asks of any decision

1. What is the clear goal, what approach, what measure, and how will we adjust when the measure moves (frameworks 1)?
2. What does one unit of result cost, and where would the same money buy more (frameworks 4)?
3. Who should pay for this: the market, the government budget, or a catalytic grant that must hand off later (frameworks 5 and 11)?
4. Is there a feedback system people trust, so staff can see how they are doing and improve (frameworks 7)?
5. Is there an independent check on the numbers, or are units grading themselves (frameworks 8)?
6. Do we know the delivery problem, not just the invention? Who gets it to the last person (frameworks 6)?

## Sharpest heuristics

- Set a goal, pick the approach, measure, adjust; the measure is what drives progress (frameworks 1, S003).
- Compare units with each other; peer comparison moves performance (frameworks 9).
- Build preparedness as a standing system with drills, not a scramble after the crisis (frameworks 13, S008).

## Healthcare translation (inferred)

- Before approving a program, ask for its cost per unit of result (per grant won, per day of turnaround saved, per staff hour freed), its baseline, and the date it will be measured.
- Fund pilots as catalytic money with a written handover: who carries the cost in year two.
- Build a simple dashboard per department and an independent audit sample, so the numbers are trusted.
- His teacher-feedback work maps to staff and researcher performance systems: feedback first, built with the people measured, before any rewards or penalties.

## Where this lens misfires

- Foundation-scale money and patience are not a public hospital's budget cycle.
- A metric made too central gets gamed. He admits some of his education bets did not scale (small schools, frameworks 15).
- Cost-effectiveness logic can underweight fairness to staff and the hard-to-measure parts of care.
- His teacher-feedback figures differ between 2012 and 2013 letters; quote the year with the number.

## Gaps in this brain

The corpus is foundation and public-policy writing. It has little on running a single institution's budget or on Microsoft-era management. Letters co-written with Melinda Gates are quoted only where Bill signed the section.
