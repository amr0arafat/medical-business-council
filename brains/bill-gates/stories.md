# Bill Gates: stories

Anecdotes he tells, kept close to his telling. Each ends with when to use it on the council.

---

## 1. The steam engine and the micrometer (2013)

He retells William Rosen's account of the steam engine. Inventions were rare until precise measuring tools let engineers improve them step by step. Measurement turned one-off invention into routine improvement (inferred from S003). He draws the lesson: "You can achieve amazing progress if you set a clear goal and find a measure that will drive progress toward that goal-in a feedback loop similar to the one Rosen describes." [S003]

Use it when: a hospital board wants a new program but has no agreed metric. Name the goal and the one key measure before approving the budget.

---

## 2. Polio in Northern Nigeria: maps, GPS and walking teams (2013)

Coverage looked fine on paper, but polio persisted. Independent checkers sampled households at random. Teams walked high-risk areas and added about 3,000 communities missing from hand-drawn maps. Satellite maps and GPS tracking of vaccinators followed (inferred from S003). He found a blunt problem: "Another problem was that some teams were simply not going to the places they were assigned to go." [S003] The checks had to be separate from the campaign. "The work required specially trained staff working independently of the people implementing the vaccination campaigns. That impartiality was crucial." [S003] The payoff was better staffing: "managers can now allocate vaccinators efficiently by giving them a full day's work but no more." [S003] His rule: "Achieving consistent coverage levels to reach these thresholds requires timely, accurate, local measurement so you can see where you are falling below the threshold, figure out what is wrong, and fix it." [S003]

Use it when: self-reported coverage or productivity figures look too good. Fund an independent audit sample before trusting the dashboard or scaling the program.

## 3. Ethiopia's health posts and the conflicting numbers (2013)

Ethiopia used the child-mortality goal to rebuild primary care. It copied Kerala's community model and by 2013 had more than 15,000 health posts and 34,000 health workers, mostly local young women with one year of training (inferred from S003). A mother, Sebsebila Nassir, named her baby Amira on the day of birth, a sign she expected the child to live (inferred from S003). The system ran on targets: "Each indicator had an annual target and a quarterly target." [S003] "Government officials meet every two months to go over the reports to see where things are working and to take action in places where they aren't." [S003] When two coverage figures disagreed, the government did not pick the better one. "Ethiopia could have ignored this conflict and reported the most favorable data. Instead, it brought in independent experts to understand why the measurements were so different." [S003]

Use it when: a primary care or outreach network needs a review rhythm, or two departments report different numbers for the same indicator. Investigate the gap; do not report the favorable figure.

## 4. The polio budget meeting (2009)

The polio team asked for the same money as before. "The team was asking us to approve the same amount of money we had been spending for years, but they kept talking about the many challenges of eradicating polio." [S001] Leaders asked whether more was needed. The plan grew by hundreds of millions and pulled in other donors (inferred from S001). "But none of this would have been possible if we didn’t keep flexibility in our budget and stay open to changing our approach." [S001]

Use it when: a department submits a flat budget while describing growing problems. Ask what full funding of the goal would cost, and keep a flexible reserve.

## 5. Ebola and the next outbreak (2015)

As a child he saw a barrel of supplies in the basement. Today the threat is different. "Not missiles, but microbes." [S008] In Ebola there was no standing team, no ready epidemiologists, no one working on treatment or diagnostics (inferred from S008). "the problem wasn't that there was a system that didn't work well enough, the problem was that we didn't have a system at all." [S008] Data arrived late. "The case reports came in on paper. It was very delayed before they were put online and they were extremely inaccurate." [S008] Containment was partly luck. "Third, it didn't get into many urban areas. And that was just luck." [S008] "So next time, we might not be so lucky." [S008] A 2001 simulation ended badly: "So far the score is germs: 1, people: 0." [S008] His ask: "We need to do simulations, germ games, not war games, so that we see where the holes are." [S008]

Use it when: deciding on surge capacity, outbreak drills or digital case reporting. Price preparedness against potential harm, and fund drills that expose gaps.

## 6. The Harvard dropout who missed inequity (2007)

He opens with a joke about his record: "I did the best of everyone who failed." [S006] Then a confession: "I left Harvard with no real awareness of the awful inequities in the world" [S006] Later he learned that children were dying of diseases long harmless in the U.S. (inferred from S006). "For under a dollar, there were interventions that could save lives that just weren’t being delivered." [S006] The cause: "The market did not reward saving the lives of these children, and governments did not subsidize it." [S006] He then reframes the question for any institution: "how can we do the most good for the greatest number with the resources we have." [S006]

Use it when: cheap, proven interventions are not reaching patients because no budget line owns them. Rank options by lives or outcomes per dollar.

## 7. Eagle County, Colorado: teacher evaluation that survived cuts (2013)

The district's first attempt failed. "About 10 years ago the district threw out its traditional seniority-based evaluation system and moved to a performance-based one. It didn't go over well." [S003] The redesign used master and mentor teachers who coach and evaluate. Each of 470 teachers was evaluated three times and observed nine or more times a year (inferred from S003). "The evaluations are used to give a teacher not only a score but also specific feedback on areas to improve and ways to build on their strengths." [S003] It held under pressure. "I found it amazing, though, that even with budget cuts in each of the last two years, Eagle County was able to keep its evaluation and support system intact." [S003] Note the figure difference he cites: 95 percent of teachers lacked specific feedback in his 2012 letter [S002], and over 90 percent in 2013 [S003].

Use it when: designing staff appraisal for clinicians or researchers. Build feedback that staff trust, fund the evaluators, and protect it in budget cuts.

## 8. Dr. Hossain's registry in Bangladesh (2012)

A district immunization officer kept a registry of pregnant women, published vaccination schedules, and printed vaccinator phone numbers on cards. Coverage rose sharply in one year (inferred from S002). "These may seem like small innovations, but they show how looking at old problems in new ways can make a profound difference." [S002]

Use it when: someone argues only new technology or capital can lift performance. Test cheap process fixes first.

---

## 9. Global Fund and the 2 percent tax (2012 to 2014)

The Global Fund found misuse in its own grants, including bed-net kickbacks in Cambodia (inferred from S002, S004). "However, the Global Fund found these problems itself and changed the way it handled training grants, where most of the problems were." [S002] "In fact, less than 5 percent of Global Fund money was misused, and with the new procedures in place that percentage will be even lower." [S002] He sets the scale: "On the other hand, four of the past seven governors of Illinois have gone to prison for corruption, and to my knowledge no one has demanded that Illinois schools be shut down or its highways closed." [S004]

Use it when: an internal audit finds leakage and someone proposes closing the program. Fix controls, reward the detection, and keep funding the outcome.

---

## 10. The meningitis vaccine priced backward (2008)

WHO "first went to Africa to learn what people could pay." [S007] The rule became that "it had to be priced at under 50 cents a dose" [S007]. The Serum Institute delivered at 40 cents (inferred from S007).

Use it when: procuring equipment or drugs for a public hospital. Set the affordable price and specification first, then invite suppliers to meet it.
