# Bill Gates: positions

Pillar: Money, impact, and measurement. Core question: What does it cost, what measurable result does it buy, and is the funding sustainable?

Source dates: S006 2007, S007 2008, S001 2009, S002 2012, S003 2013, S004 2014, S008 2015, S005 2020, S009 2023. S004 and S005 are joint letters; only sections the notes mark as Bill's are quoted. LATEST marks his most recent stated view. CHANGE marks a shift over time.

---

## 1. Measurement and evaluation

**Pick the key variable and use it as feedback (2013).** His clearest model is a loop: goal, key measure, action, feedback, adjust (inferred from S003). "You use the measurement as feedback to make adjustments. I think a lot of efforts fail because they don't focus on the right measure or they don't invest enough in doing it accurately." [S003]

**Public bodies must build their own feedback loop (2009).** A company gets profit as its goal. A nonprofit or government must choose its own goal and measure (inferred from S003). "You don’t have a stock price that goes up and down to tell you how you’re doing." [S001] "This lack of a natural feedback loop means that we as a foundation have to be even more careful in picking our goals and being honest with ourselves when we are not achieving them." [S001]

**Earlier form: see, solve, show (2007).** "To turn caring into action, we need to see a problem, see a solution, and see the impact. But complexity blocks all three steps." [S006] The third step, he says, "is to measure the impact of your work and share your successes and failures so that others learn from your efforts." [S006]

**Make bad news safe, and watch for gaming (2013).** "You have to measure accurately, as well as create an environment where problems can be discussed openly so you can effectively evaluate what's working and what's not." [S003] "Setting targets for immunization and other interventions can motivate government health workers, but it can also encourage over-reporting to avoid problems with supervisors." [S003] His fix is an independent check layer (see stories.md, Nigeria and Ethiopia).

**Few goals, peer comparison (2013).** "adding many new goals, or goals that are not easily measurable, may sap momentum." [S003] "This is one of the benefits of measurement-the ability it gives government leaders to make comparisons across countries, find who's doing well, and then learn from the best." [S003]

**Credibility comes from evidence (2009).** "Since we are in this for the long run, we need to develop credibility by the strength of our evidence, and by not claiming to know more than we do." [S001]

**Efficacy is not effectiveness (2012, again 2020).** "In studies where the patients used the tool as they were supposed to, the results were quite good. However, in most studies the levels of usage were low and thus the overall results were disappointing." [S002] In 2020, on daily HIV prevention pills: "Local health programs have struggled to deliver a daily pill in a way that’s appealing and fits into people’s lives." [S005]

**Leading indicators (2020, LATEST).** Ninth-grade on-track status "is more predictive of whether that student will graduate than race, wealth, or even test scores." [S005]

## 2. Health systems, primary care, vaccines and delivery

**Vaccines are the best buy (2009).** "Nothing on the planet saves children’s lives more effectively and inexpensively than vaccines." [S001] New products carry system costs: "So adding a new vaccine, like one for rotavirus, that needs a lot of refrigerator space requires increasing the refrigeration capacity at every stage of the entire delivery chain" [S001]

**Delivery is the bottleneck (2012 to 2013).** "Vaccines are the only high-technology product that needs to be delivered to every single child. To miss zero children, it takes an incredible amount of ingenuity" [S002] "We have to develop and test overall delivery systems, including communication, support, and incentives, in ways that go beyond what a medical trial alone typically does." [S002] "This innovation to reduce the delivery bottleneck is critical." [S003]

**Primary care on targets (2013).** Ethiopia built more than 15,000 health posts staffed by 34,000 health workers (inferred from S003). Measurement systems built for one program help others: "The measurement systems put in place by the eradication initiative will be invaluable for other health care activities" [S003]

**Pooled buying lowers price (2020, LATEST).** "By 2019, Gavi had helped vaccinate more than 760 million children and prevent 13 million deaths." [S005] "a single dose of the pentavalent vaccine, which protects against five deadly infections, used to cost $3.65. It now costs less than a dollar." [S005]

**Gains decay when attention moves (2013).** "Sadly, some of the gains didn't last. Once the vaccination goal was reached, donor attention shifted elsewhere and coverage rates drifted down in many countries." [S003]

## 3. Funding models: aid, budgets, philanthropy and markets

**Convert money into outcomes (2009 to 2014).** "With a handful of new vaccines, we should be able to save a year of a person’s life for well under $100. If we waste $500,000, we are wasting 5,000 years of life." [S001] "That will mean every $300 that governments invest in the Global Fund will put another person on treatment for a year. Every $300 that’s not forthcoming will represent a person taken off treatment. That’s a very clear choice." [S002] On all health aid since 1980: "It comes to less than $5,000 per child saved" [S004] He names the bias: "This calculation does not take into account how child mortality might have declined without aid, which would increase the cost per life saved." [S004]

**Aid is a bargain (2014, LATEST on aid).** "Health aid is a phenomenal investment." [S004] "Particularly in health and agriculture, we can validate the outcomes and know the value we’re getting per dollar spent." [S004]

**Effectiveness demands are fair (2013).** "Given how tight budgets are around the world, governments are rightfully demanding effectiveness in the programs they pay for." [S003]

**Fight waste, keep proportion (2014).** "It is true that when health aid is stolen or wasted, it costs lives. We need to root out fraud and squeeze more out of every dollar." [S004] "Suppose small-scale corruption amounts to a 2 percent tax on the cost of saving a life. We should try to reduce that. But if we can’t, should we stop trying to save lives?" [S004]

**Who funds what. CHANGE from 2008 to 2009.** In 2008 the frame is markets versus caring: "Capitalism harnesses self-interest in a helpful and sustainable way, but only on behalf of those who can pay. Government aid and philanthropy channel our caring for those who can’t pay." [S007] By 2009 he draws hard lines for the foundation. "Foundations are not needed in areas where capitalistic market signals work well and the poorest aren’t left out." [S001] "This is why we tend not to fund other important things like building health clinics or roads, which are better left to governments." [S001]

**Creative capitalism (2007 to 2008).** "economic demand is not the same as economic need." [S007] "In markets where profits are not possible, recognition is a proxy; where profits are possible, recognition is an added incentive." [S007] "I believe the highest-leverage work that governments can do is to set policy to create market incentives for business activity that improves the lives of the poor." [S007] His tools: advance market commitments, priority review vouchers and tiered pricing (inferred from S007).

**Spend for payback, keep budgets flexible (2009).** "the goal of our foundation is to make investments whose payback to society is very high rather than to pay out the minimum to make the endowment last as long as possible." [S001] Finishing pays a dividend: "Once we get rid of polio, the world will save about $2 billion a year that it now spends fighting the disease." [S004]

**Sustainability: countries take over (2014).** "Many low- and middle-income countries will develop enough to pay for this convergence themselves. Others will need continued generosity from donors, including investments in health-related R&D." [S004] Middle-income countries should fund health through tobacco taxes and fewer fossil-fuel subsidies (inferred from S004).

## 4. Pandemics and preparedness (2015, LATEST)

"But we've actually invested very little in a system to stop an epidemic." [S008] He wants a standing system: strong health systems, a medical reserve corps, military logistics, simulations and R&D on vaccines and diagnostics (inferred from S008). "First, we need strong health systems in poor countries." [S008] He gives no line-item cost. "Now I don't have an exact budget for what this would cost, but I'm quite sure it's very modest compared to the potential harm." [S008] Primary care doubles as surveillance: "But, also where we'll see the outbreak very early on." [S008]

## 5. Education measurement (CHANGE, 2009 to 2020)

- 2009: small schools failed. "Many of the small schools that we invested in did not improve students’ achievement in any significant way." [S001] Focus moves to teachers: "If you want your child to get the best education possible, it is actually more important to get him assigned to a great teacher than to a great school." [S001]
- 2012 and 2013: teacher feedback. **His figure differs between letters.** 2012: "I still find it hard to believe that 95 percent of teachers are not given specific feedback about how to improve." [S002] 2013: "I was amazed to learn a few years ago that over 90 percent of teachers get zero feedback on how to improve." [S003] Cite the figure with its year.
- 2012: the cost is the seriousness test. "Dedicating 2 percent of teachers to do this work is a large investment. It can mean raising the average class size by 2 percent or spending 2 percent more money." [S002] "I see the willingness to make this investment as a test of whether people are serious about an evaluation system that really works." [S002]
- 2013: "I think the most critical change we can make in U.S. K-12 education is to create teacher feedback systems like the one in Eagle County that are properly funded, high quality, and trusted by teachers." [S003]
- 2020 (LATEST): scale is the problem. "Once projects expanded to reach hundreds of thousands of students, we stopped seeing the results we hoped for." [S005] "scaling in education doesn’t mean getting the same solution out to everyone." [S005] "We thought that if states raised the standards the market would respond and develop new instructional materials that aligned with those standards. That didn’t happen, so we looked for ways to encourage the market." [S005]

## 6. AI in health (2023, LATEST)

AI will take on "filing insurance claims, dealing with paperwork, and drafting notes from a doctor’s visit." [S009] "AIs will help the health workers they do see be more productive." [S009] Markets will not aim it at the poor: "market forces won’t naturally produce AI products and services that help the poorest. The opposite is more likely." [S009] "With reliable funding and the right policies, governments and philanthropy can ensure that AIs are used to reduce inequity." [S009]

He weighs regulation against access. "AIs have to be tested very carefully and properly regulated, which means it will take longer for them to be adopted than in other areas. But then again, humans make mistakes too. And having no access to medical care is also a problem." [S009] "People will need to see evidence that health AIs are beneficial overall, even though they won’t be perfect and will make mistakes." [S009] "The AI models used in poor countries will need to be trained on different diseases than in rich countries." [S009] "But the demand for people who help other people will never go away." [S009]

## 7. Arc, 2007 to 2023

- 2007 to 2008: inequity is a market and government failure; answer with creative capitalism (inferred from S006, S007).
- 2009 to 2014: foundation discipline. Cost per outcome, goal-measure-adjust, aid as a bargain (inferred from S001 to S004).
- 2015: from programs to standing outbreak systems (inferred from S008).
- 2020: humility about scale in education and daily HIV pills (inferred from S005).
- 2023: AI as the new tool; equity needs public and philanthropic money (inferred from S009).
