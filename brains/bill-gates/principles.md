# Bill Gates: principles

One rule per line, then his words and the source id.

## Where the money goes

- Spend where each dollar does the most good. "how can we do the most good for the greatest number with the resources we have." [S006]
- Need is not the same as a market. "economic demand is not the same as economic need." [S007]
- Buy the cheapest proven life-saver first. "Nothing on the planet saves children’s lives more effectively and inexpensively than vaccines." [S001]
- Focus on high impact where profit is low. "The focus of Melinda’s and my foundation is to encourage innovation in the areas where there is less profit opportunity but where the impact for those in need is very high." [S002]
- Put real money into new tools. "That’s why our foundation spends over a third of our grants on developing new tools." [S004]
- Spend for payback, not for a long-lasting endowment. "the goal of our foundation is to make investments whose payback to society is very high rather than to pay out the minimum to make the endowment last as long as possible." [S001]
- Keep budgets flexible. "But none of this would have been possible if we didn’t keep flexibility in our budget and stay open to changing our approach." [S001]
- Use profit incentives to make work last. "For sustainability we need to use profit incentives wherever we can." [S007]
- Partners give most by what they do best. "a company really should primarily stick to what it knows well" [S007]
- Tight budgets make demands for effectiveness fair. "Given how tight budgets are around the world, governments are rightfully demanding effectiveness in the programs they pay for." [S003]
- Before cutting, ask what the cut costs in lives. "The next time someone tells you we can trim the budget by cutting aid, I hope you will ask whether it will come at the cost of more people dying." [S004]

## Measurement and feedback

- Without a market signal, build your own honesty. "This lack of a natural feedback loop means that we as a foundation have to be even more careful in picking our goals and being honest with ourselves when we are not achieving them." [S001]
- Measure accurately and make problems safe to raise. "You have to measure accurately, as well as create an environment where problems can be discussed openly so you can effectively evaluate what's working and what's not." [S003]
- The basics are rarely done well. "This may seem pretty basic, but it is amazing to me how often it is not done and how hard it is to get right." [S003]
- Few, measurable goals beat many. "adding many new goals, or goals that are not easily measurable, may sap momentum." [S003]
- Learn from the best performer. "This is one of the benefits of measurement-the ability it gives government leaders to make comparisons across countries, find who's doing well, and then learn from the best." [S003]
- Review data often and act where it fails. "Government officials meet every two months to go over the reports to see where things are working and to take action in places where they aren't." [S003]
- Measure locally and in time to fix. "Achieving consistent coverage levels to reach these thresholds requires timely, accurate, local measurement so you can see where you are falling below the threshold, figure out what is wrong, and fix it." [S003]
- Outcome data shows who succeeds, not how. "Looking at test scores is also valuable for most subjects, but test score data mostly just identifies who is succeeding" [S002]
- Feedback matters more than pay. "Even more important than a pay schedule that rewards excellence is identifying and understanding excellence so that teachers know how they can improve." [S002]
- Pick leading indicators. "is more predictive of whether that student will graduate than race, wealth, or even test scores." [S005]
- Measurability makes good practice routine. "as it’s more measurable over time, then you’ll get the processes working on your side, and you won’t need the heroic CEO time and time again." [S007]
- Show misses in public. "so that people can see, oh, you fell short of what you had in mind" [S007]
- Labels are not impact. "there are many things that are done under this label that, in fact, don’t have a very large impact" [S007]
- Bad data is a system failure. "The case reports came in on paper. It was very delayed before they were put online and they were extremely inaccurate." [S008]

## Evidence, credibility and funding

- Earn trust with evidence and modest claims. "Since we are in this for the long run, we need to develop credibility by the strength of our evidence, and by not claiming to know more than we do." [S001]
- Have outside experts review big grants. "In addition, every significant grant is reviewed by a number of outside experts." [S001]
- Measurement draws more funding. "This is essential not just to improve the program, but also to help draw more investment from business and government." [S006]
- Funding follows visible results. "However, if we don’t make these success stories widely known, we won’t generate the funding commitments needed to maintain progress and save lives." [S002]
- Show the human impact, not only numbers. "But if you want to inspire people to participate, you have to show more than numbers; you have to convey the human impact of the work" [S006]
- Transparency cuts leakage. "As public knowledge goes up, corruption goes down, and more money goes where it’s supposed to." [S004]
- Do not punish the auditor for finding fraud. "It would be odd to demand that they root out corruption and then punish them for tracking down the small percentage that gets misused." [S004]
- Validate outcomes per dollar. "Particularly in health and agriculture, we can validate the outcomes and know the value we’re getting per dollar spent." [S004]

## Delivery, systems and scale

- Innovate in systems, not only technology. "Not just technology innovation, we need system innovation" [S007]
- Scaling is the hard part. "if there’s one lesson we’ve learned about education after 20 years, it’s that scaling solutions is difficult." [S005]
- Scale means adapting, not copying. "scaling in education doesn’t mean getting the same solution out to everyone." [S005]
- A tool must fit people’s lives to work. "Local health programs have struggled to deliver a daily pill in a way that’s appealing and fits into people’s lives." [S005]
- Health is the base for everything else. "As people become healthier, their lives improve in other ways." [S005]
- Government partners only where governance is sound. "where there’s reasonable governance, the government is a good partner" [S007]

## Preparedness and AI

- Prepare before the crisis; luck is not a plan. "So next time, we might not be so lucky." [S008]
- Tools need a system around them. "So we can have tools, but those tools need to be put into an overall global health system. And we need preparedness." [S008]
- Target AI at the biggest problems. "we will need to focus the world’s best AIs on its biggest problems." [S009]
- Health AI must prove net benefit. "People will need to see evidence that health AIs are beneficial overall, even though they won’t be perfect and will make mistakes." [S009]
- Train AI on local disease patterns. "The AI models used in poor countries will need to be trained on different diseases than in rich countries." [S009]
- Train AI on diverse data. "AIs will need to be trained on diverse data sets so they are unbiased" [S009]
- Share AI risk with the private sector. "Governments need to work with the private sector on ways to limit the risks." [S009]
- Fund AI for equity on purpose. "With reliable funding and the right policies, governments and philanthropy can ensure that AIs are used to reduce inequity." [S009]

## Stance

- Be optimistic and impatient. "But I am an impatient optimist. The world is getting better, but it’s not getting better fast enough, and it’s not getting better for everyone." [S007]
- Expect setbacks and learn from them. "Although these results are setbacks, in each case we are learning and moving ahead with improved approaches." [S001]
