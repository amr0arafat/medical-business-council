# Jeff Bezos: positions

Pillar: Strategy and decisions. Core question: Is this the right bet for the institution, and how reversible is it?

Sources span 1997 to 2023. Dates below are publication dates. Quotes tagged (1997 letter) come from the 1997 shareholder letter reprinted in S001. The latest material is S009 (Lex Fridman interview, December 2023). S007 is a statement prepared for an antitrust hearing, so its market and jobs figures are advocacy, not neutral analysis.

---

## 1. Decisions and meetings

**Sort every decision by reversibility first (2016, restated 2017 and 2023; latest 2023).**
- "Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or nearly irreversible" [S001]
- "these decisions must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation." [S001]
- "Type 2 decisions can and should be made quickly by high judgment individuals or small groups." [S001]
- 2023 restatement: "Changing your mind, I talk about one-way doors and two-way doors, most decisions are two-way doors." [S009]
- "Two-way door decisions should mostly be made by single individuals or by very small teams deep in the organization." [S009]
- One-way doors go up: "Those are the ones that should be elevated up to the senior-most executives who should slow them down and make sure that the right thing is being done." [S009]
- The senior role on big bets: "I often found myself in the position of being the chief slow down officer" [S009]
- Both errors matter. "Any companies that habitually use the light-weight Type 2 decision-making process to make Type 1 decisions go extinct before they get large." [S001]
- A reversible decision that is very costly or slow to reverse should still be treated as one-way (inferred from S009).

Change over time: in 2016 it was Type 1 and Type 2. By 2023 the language is doors, and he adds a new image for technical lock-in: "some technical decisions are like quick-drying cement. Once you make them, it gets really hard." [S009]

**Decide with about 70% of the information (2017).**
- "most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had." [S002]
- "If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're probably being slow." [S002]
- "If you're good at course correcting, being wrong may be less costly than you think, whereas being slow is going to be expensive for sure." [S002]

**Disagree and commit, including the boss (2017; restated 2023, see stories.md).**
- "This isn't one way. If you're the boss, you should do this too. I disagree and commit all the time." [S002]
- "It's a genuine disagreement of opinion, a candid expression of my view, a chance for the team to weigh my view, and a quick, sincere commitment to go their way." [S002]

**Settle disputes by truth, then escalation. Never by compromise or exhaustion (2017, 2023; latest 2023).**
- "Without escalation, the default dispute resolution mechanism for this scenario is exhaustion. Whoever has more stamina carries the decision." [S002]
- "you shouldn’t allow compromise to be used when you can know the truth." [S009]
- "Exhausting the other person is not truth seeking." [S009]
- Order: measure if the truth is knowable; if not, disagree and commit; if stuck, escalate (inferred from S009). The 2023 version adds the explicit attack on compromise, which the 2017 letter did not have.

**Write a memo, read it together, then let the meeting wander (2018, 2023; latest 2023).**
- "We don't do PowerPoint (or any other slide-oriented) presentations at Amazon." [S003]
- "Instead, we write narratively structured six-page memos." [S003]
- "For 30 minutes, we sit there silently together in the meeting and read." [S009]
- "you can hide a lot of sloppy thinking behind bullet points." [S009]
- "I like a crisp document and a messy meeting." [S009]
- Business reviews are different: "Those meetings can be very efficient. They can start on time and end on time." [S009]

**Protect truth-telling in the room (2023, latest).**
- "in every meeting that I attend, I always speak last." [S009]
- "you want to set up your culture so that the most junior person can overrule the most senior person if they have data." [S009]

**Doubt the metric when the story disagrees (2017, 2023; latest 2023).**
- "when the data and the anecdotes disagree, the anecdotes are usually right." [S009]
- "And by the way, you do need metrics." [S009]

## 2. Process and bureaucracy

**The process is not the thing (2017).**
- "Good process serves you so you can serve customers. But if you're not watchful, the process can become the thing." [S002]
- "The process is not the thing. It's always worth asking, do we own the process or does the process own us?" [S002]
- When a bad outcome is defended by citing process: "A more experienced leader will use it as an opportunity to investigate and improve the process." [S002]

**Large organizations drift to heavy process on everything (2016, 2023).**
- "As organizations get larger, there seems to be a tendency to use the heavy-weight Type 1 decision-making process on most decisions, including many Type 2 decisions." [S001]
- "The end result of this is slowness, unthoughtful risk aversion, failure to experiment sufficiently, and consequently diminished invention." [S001]
- "It’s very common, especially in large companies, that they’re managing to metrics that they don’t really understand." [S009]

**Small teams with single owners; separate teams for small defects (2016, 2023).**
- "AWS is made up of many small teams with single-threaded owners, enabling rapid innovation." [S001]
- "the teams working on the big issues never get to the paper cuts." [S009]

**Tenets, but no dogma (2023, latest).**
- "you never want to get trapped by dogma. You never want to get trapped by history." [S009]

## 3. Long-term bets and failure

**Long term over short term (1997, held through 2023).**
- "We will continue to make investment decisions in light of long-term market leadership considerations rather than short-term profitability considerations or short-term Wall Street reactions." [S001] (1997 letter)
- "to jettison those that do not provide acceptable returns, and to step up our investment in those that work best." [S001] (1997 letter)
- "Long-term thinking is a giant lever." [S009]
- "Five years is a tough timeframe for most institutions to think past." [S009]
- Plan around what is stable: "think about the things that are not going to change over 10 years. And those are probably the big things." [S009]
- Change over time: the 1997 frame is market leadership. By 2023 he pushes horizons out to 10 to 25 years (inferred from S009).

**Failure is the price of invention, and it must scale (2016, 2019, 2020).**
- "failure and invention are inseparable twins." [S001]
- "Given a ten percent chance of a 100 times payoff, you should take that bet every time. But you're still going to be wrong nine times out of ten." [S001]
- "If the size of your failures isn't growing, you're not going to be inventing at a size that can actually move the needle." [S004]
- "Of course, we won't undertake such experiments cavalierly. We will work hard to make them good bets, but not all good bets will ultimately pay out." [S004]
- "Outsized returns come from betting against conventional wisdom, but conventional wisdom is usually right." [S007]

**Realism and optimism together (2023, latest).**
- "Every startup company is unlikely to work. It’s helpful to be in reality about that, but that doesn’t mean you can’t be optimistic." [S009]

**Overclaim to flag.** In 2021 he wrote "We have never failed when we set our minds to something" [S006]. Treat this as an overclaim. It conflicts with his own record of named failures (Fire phone, Auctions, zShops, early ML tools) in S001, S003 and S004.

## 4. People and workplace

**Hire high, think like owners (1997).**
- "Setting the bar high in our approach to hiring has been, and will continue to be, the single most important element of Amazon.com's success." [S001] (1997 letter)
- "each of whom must think like, and therefore must actually be, an owner." [S001] (1997 letter)

**High standards are teachable and need realistic scope (2018).**
- "they are teachable, they are domain specific, you must recognize them, and you must explicitly coach realistic scope." [S003]
- "You can consider yourself a person of high standards in general and still have debilitating blind spots." [S003]
- "Someone on the team needs to have the skill, but it doesn't have to be you." [S003]

**Pay and benefits (2016 to 2021).**
- Benefits are the same for frontline and senior staff: "these benefits are egalitarian" [S001]
- $15 minimum wage: "We did it because it seemed like the right thing to do." [S004]
- COVID wage increases: "While we recognize this is expensive, we believe it's the right thing to do under the circumstances." [S005]

**A shift in 2021: employees become a second vision (latest letter).**
- "I think we need to do a better job for our employees." [S006]
- "it's clear to me that we need a better vision for how we create value for employees" [S006]
- "We are going to be Earth's Best Employer and Earth's Safest Place to Work." [S006]
- On the Bessemer union vote: "Does your Chair take comfort in the outcome of the recent union vote in Bessemer? No, he doesn't." [S006]
- This is the clearest change over time. The 1997 to 2019 letters frame people mostly through hiring and ownership. The 2021 letter admits a gap and commits to fix it (inferred from S006).

## 5. Institutions and scrutiny

**All large institutions should be scrutinized (2020).**
- "We should scrutinize all large institutions, whether they’re companies, government agencies, or non-profits." [S007]
- "Our responsibility is to make sure we pass such scrutiny with flying colors." [S007]
- Advocacy note: S007 also argues Amazon is a small share of retail. Those figures were made before an antitrust panel and are advocacy (inferred from S007).

**Listen to critics, but hold firm when right (2020).**
- "When we’re criticized for those choices, we listen and look at ourselves in the mirror. When we think our critics are right, we change. When we make mistakes, we apologize." [S007]
- "But when you look in the mirror, assess the criticism, and still believe you’re doing the right thing, no force in the world should be able to move you." [S007]

**Scale is useful, but it can slow you (2016).**
- "there are certain things that only large companies can do." [S001]
- "if we're not vigilant and thoughtful, size could slow us down and diminish our inventiveness." [S001]
- "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful decline. Followed by death. And that is why it is always Day 1." [S002]

**Trust is earned slowly (2020).**
- Amazon ranked behind only physicians and the military in one trust survey: "Only their primary physicians and the military, according to a January 2020 Morning Consult survey." [S007] (advocacy figure)

## 6. Health and safety

**COVID testing and the in-house lab (April 2020).**
- "If every person could be tested regularly, it would make a huge difference in how we fight this virus." [S005]
- "Those who test positive could be quarantined and cared for, and everyone who tests negative could re-enter the economy with confidence." [S005]
- He built an in-house testing lab as a bounded experiment and promised to share what was learned (inferred from S005). See stories.md.
- Safety in operations: "we've made over 150 significant process changes in our operations network" [S005]
- Medical experts and health authorities were consulted, and measures were audited daily (inferred from S005).

**Workplace injury as a health problem (2021, latest letter).**
- "We need to invent solutions to reduce MSDs for new employees" [S006]
- Musculoskeletal disorders were about 40% of work injuries. Job rotation and coaching programs followed (inferred from S006).

**Safety engineering and risk tolerance (2023, latest).**
- "it is impossible to ever be sure that nothing will go wrong." [S009]
- "And so the only way to improve safety is to have an escape system." [S009]
- "There are human activities where we tolerate more risk if you’re saving somebody’s life, if you are engaging in real exploration." [S009]

**Health care as a future student of customer focus (2023).**
- "one day, people are going to come to Amazon from the healthcare industry" [S009]
- Career Choice funds healthcare training "regardless of whether it’s relevant to a career at Amazon." [S007]
