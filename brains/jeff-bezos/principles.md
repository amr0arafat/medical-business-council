# Principles: Jeff Bezos

One rule per line, then his words. S007 is testimony prepared for an antitrust hearing; read its claims as advocacy. Quotes from the 1997 letter are cited to S001 and tagged (1997 letter).

## Reversibility and decision weight

- Most doors swing both ways; use a light process on them. "Many decisions are reversible, two-way doors. Those decisions can use a light-weight process." [S002]
- On a reversible call, the cost of being wrong is small. "For those, so what if you're wrong?" [S002]
- A bad two-way decision can be undone. "You can reopen the door and go back through." [S001]
- Let capable individuals or small groups make Type 2 calls. "Type 2 decisions can and should be made quickly by high judgment individuals or small groups." [S001]
- Default to one decider who can change course. "Most decisions should be made by single individuals and done quickly in the full understanding that you can always change your mind." [S009]
- Push ownership into small teams. "AWS is made up of many small teams with single-threaded owners, enabling rapid innovation." [S001]

## Speed

- Speed is a value in itself. "Speed matters in business" [S002]
- Move as fast as responsibility allows. "moving forward quickly on decisions, as quickly as you responsibly can is how you increase velocity." [S009]
- Study the options, but briefly. "If there are five ways to do something, we’ll study them, but let’s study them very quickly and make a decision." [S009]
- Speed depends on fixing mistakes fast. "you need to be good at quickly recognizing and correcting bad decisions." [S002]
- Keep urgency even when things go well. "Though we are optimistic, we must remain vigilant and maintain a sense of urgency." [S001] (1997 letter)

## Disagreement and escalation

- Defer to whoever is closer to the facts. "I don’t think you’re right, but I’m going to gamble with you and you’re closer to the ground truth than I am." [S009]
- Once committed, help it succeed. "I’m going to try actively to help make sure it works. That’s a really important teammate behavior." [S009]
- Escalate deep misalignment at once. "recognize true misalignment issues early and escalate them immediately." [S002]
- Do not win by wearing people down. "Exhausting the other person is not truth seeking." [S009]
- Escalation beats attrition. "escalation is better than war of attrition." [S009]

## Truth-telling in meetings

- Build mechanisms for candor. "any high performing organization has to have mechanisms and a culture that supports truth-telling." [S009]
- Data outranks rank. "you want to set up your culture so that the most junior person can overrule the most senior person if they have data." [S009]
- The senior person speaks last. "if you’re the most senior person in the room, go last, let everybody else go first." [S009]
- Distrust the comfortable reading. "it’s a little dangerous to jump to the conclusion that the happy interpretation is right." [S009]
- Seek truth when it hurts. "you have to seek truth even when it’s uncomfortable" [S009]
- Respect hunches. "a lot of our most powerful truths turn out to be hunches" [S009]

## Memos and meetings

- Memos are hard for the writer and easy for the reader. "it’s easy for the author and hard for the audience. And a memo is the opposite." [S009]
- Full sentences expose weak logic. "When you have to write in complete sentences with narrative structure, it’s really hard to hide sloppy thinking." [S009]
- Good memos take time. "A good six-page memo might take two weeks to write." [S009]
- Problem-solving meetings should explore. "the meeting is about asking questions that nobody knows the answer to and trying to wander your way to a solution." [S009]

## Proxies, metrics, and process

- Remember why each metric exists. "you forget the truth behind why you were watching that metric in the first place." [S009]
- Surveys are not the customer. "market research and customer surveys can become proxies for customers" [S002]
- Past success is not a rule. "There’s so much value in what has worked in the past, but you can’t be blindly following what you’ve done." [S009]
- Even consistency can mislead. "Self-consistency even can be a trap." [S009]
- Give small defects their own team. "the teams working on the big issues never get to the paper cuts." [S009]
- Ride big trends, do not fight them. "If you fight them, you're probably fighting the future. Embrace them and you have a tailwind." [S002]

## High standards

- Know what good looks like first. "First, you have to be able to recognize what good looks like in that domain." [S003]
- Teach realistic scope. "you can improve results through the simple act of teaching scope" [S003]
- Standards cover unseen work. "The work that gets done when no one is watching." [S003]
- Hire with a high bar. "Setting the bar high in our approach to hiring has been, and will continue to be, the single most important element of Amazon.com's success." [S001] (1997 letter)

## Customers and trust

- Watch customers, not rivals. "Most big technology companies are competitor focused. They see what others are doing, and then work to fast follow." [S001]
- Expectations keep rising. "yesterday's 'wow' quickly becomes today's 'ordinary'." [S003]
- Ask, listen, then deliver. "It's critical to ask customers what they want, listen carefully to their answers, and figure out a plan to provide it thoughtfully and quickly" [S004]
- Trust is fragile. "Customer trust is hard to win and easy to lose." [S007]
- Trust is earned through hard work over time. "You earn trust slowly, over time, by doing hard things well" [S007]

## Long-term bets and capital

- Be bold where the odds justify it. "We will make bold rather than timid investment decisions where we see a sufficient probability of gaining market leadership advantages." [S001] (1997 letter)
- Cut what fails, fund what works. "to jettison those that do not provide acceptable returns, and to step up our investment in those that work best." [S001] (1997 letter)
- Prioritizing is the hard part. "a big part of the challenge for us will lie not in finding new ways to expand our business, but in prioritizing our investments." [S001] (1997 letter)
- Real cash value beats accounting looks. "When forced to choose between optimizing the appearance of our GAAP accounting and maximizing the present value of future cash flows, we'll take the cash flows." [S001] (1997 letter)
- Spend wisely. "We will work hard to spend wisely and maintain our lean culture." [S001] (1997 letter)
- Be explicit about uncertainty in estimates. "acknowledging up front that the error bars are high." [S006]

## Experiments and failure

- Big institutions must accept failed experiments. "Most large organizations embrace the idea of invention, but are not willing to suffer the string of failed experiments necessary to get there." [S001]
- Failures should scale with the institution. "As a company grows, everything needs to scale, including the size of your failed experiments." [S004]
- One win can pay for many losses. "a single big winning bet can more than cover the cost of many losers." [S004]
- Protect young ideas. "Staying in Day 1 requires you to experiment patiently, accept failures, plant seeds, protect saplings, and double down when you see customer delight." [S002]
- Be realistic and optimistic at once. "Every startup company is unlikely to work. It’s helpful to be in reality about that, but that doesn’t mean you can’t be optimistic." [S009]
- Efficiency and invention pull apart. "Efficiency and invention are sort of at odds" [S009]
- Use intuition with analysis. "we use not just analysis but also intuition and heart to find our way forward." [S004]
- Safety needs a way out, not a promise. "And so the only way to improve safety is to have an escape system." [S009]

## Conviction and scrutiny

- Firm on vision, flexible on details. "On the details, we at Amazon are always flexible, but on matters of vision we are stubborn and relentless." [S006]
- Listen to critics and change when they are right. "When we think our critics are right, we change. When we make mistakes, we apologize." [S007]
- After honest review, hold your ground. "But when you look in the mirror, assess the criticism, and still believe you’re doing the right thing, no force in the world should be able to move you." [S007]
- All large institutions deserve scrutiny. "We should scrutinize all large institutions, whether they’re companies, government agencies, or non-profits." [S007]
- Distinctiveness has a cost worth paying. "You have to pay a price for your distinctiveness, and it's worth it." [S006]
- Regret what you did not try. "And most of our regrets are acts of omission" [S007]
