## Richard Dawkins (S006)

In his last letter as CEO, he borrows a biology passage from Dawkins. The passage itself is not his, so it is not quoted here. Its idea is that living things work to avoid falling into equilibrium with their surroundings (inferred from S006). He uses it as a metaphor for staying distinct.

He extends it beyond biology:

- "I would argue that it's relevant to all companies and all institutions and to each of our individual lives too." [S006]
- "How much work does it take to maintain your distinctiveness?" [S006]
- "The world wants you to be typical" [S006]
- "Being yourself is worth it, but don't expect it to be easy or free. You'll have to put energy into it continuously." [S006]

He applies the same idea to political institutions: "Democracies are not normal. Tyranny is the historical norm." [S006]

Council use: a public hospital or research center keeps its distinct mission only through steady effort. Without it, the institution drifts toward the average of its surroundings.
