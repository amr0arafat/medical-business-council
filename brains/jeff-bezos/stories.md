# Jeff Bezos: stories

Anecdotes he tells in his own sources. Quotes are exact. Retellings without quotes are marked as inferred.

---

## 1. The Fire phone and the billions in failures (2016 to 2020)

The Fire phone failed. He does not hide it. "While the Fire phone was a failure, we were able to take our learnings (as well as the developers) and accelerate our efforts building Echo and Alexa." [S004] He tells it as part of a longer list. "We took two big swings and missed" [S001] with Auctions and zShops. "We learned from our failures and stayed stubborn on the vision" [S001]. Early ML tools for outside customers also flopped: "our initial attempts to externalize some of our early internal Machine Learning tools were failures." [S004] His summary: "We've also had billions of dollars' worth of failures along the way." [S003] The lesson he draws is that one winner pays for many losers: "a single big winning bet can more than cover the cost of many losers." [S004]

Use it when: a pilot program (a new clinic line, a digital tool, a research core) has failed and leaders must decide whether to write it off or carry its people and lessons into the next attempt.

## 2. Regret minimization and leaving the hedge fund (told 2010 and 2020)

Before Amazon, he read a striking number. "I came across the fact that Web usage was growing at 2,300% per year. I had never seen or heard of anything that grew that fast." [S008] He told his boss at D. E. Shaw he wanted to leave. The boss walked him through Central Park and said the idea was better for someone without a good job (inferred from S008). "That logic made some sense to me and he convinced me to think about it for 48 hours before making a final decision." [S008] He used a simple test. "When I’m 80 and reflecting back, I want to have minimized the number of regrets that I have in my life." [S007] "I didn’t think I’d regret trying and failing. And I suspected I would always be haunted by a decision to not try at all." [S008] He admits: "It was a decision I made with my heart and not my head." [S007] Note: this is a personal, career-level frame, not a capital-budgeting tool.

Use it when: a senior clinician or administrator is weighing whether to leave a secure post to lead a new program, or a board must give a hesitant leader a short cooling-off period before a one-way choice.

## 3. The grandmother and the smoking arithmetic (Princeton, 2010)

At about age 10 he did the math on how much his grandmother's smoking was costing her. "At that age, I’d take any excuse to make estimates and do minor arithmetic." [S008] He told her she had lost about 9 years (inferred from S008). She cried. His grandfather pulled over and told him it is harder to be kind than clever (inferred from S008; the grandfather's words are not quoted as Bezos's). The line Bezos drew from it: "Cleverness is a gift. Kindness is a choice." [S008]

Use it when: a team has correct numbers on a sensitive topic (mortality data, staff cuts, a unit closure) and must decide how to present them to patients, families, or staff.

## 4. The handstand coach (2018 letter)

Someone learning a handstand from a coach expected it to take two weeks. The coach said it takes about six months of daily practice (inferred from S003; the coach's words are not his). He applies it to work: people fail at high standards when they hold unrealistic beliefs about scope. "They're trying to perfect a handstand in just two weeks, and we're not coaching them right." [S003] "you can improve results through the simple act of teaching scope" [S003] The same goes for memos: "The great memos are written and re-written, shared with colleagues who are asked to improve the work, set aside for a couple of days, and then edited again with a fresh mind." [S003]

Use it when: setting a timeline for accreditation, an EHR rollout, or a quality program, and staff assume it can be done in weeks when it needs months.

## 5. Disagree and commit on an Amazon Studios show (2017 letter)

A team wanted to make an original series. He thought it was debatable, complicated to produce, and on weak business terms (inferred from S002). The team felt otherwise. He wrote back: "I disagree and commit and hope it becomes the most watched thing we've ever made." [S002] His point is speed: "Consider how much slower this decision cycle would have been if the team had actually had to convince me rather than simply get my commitment." [S002] In 2023 he gave the inner script: "I don’t think you’re right, but I’m going to gamble with you and you’re closer to the ground truth than I am." [S009] Then: "I’m going to try actively to help make sure it works. That’s a really important teammate behavior." [S009]

Use it when: a department head or principal investigator, closer to the work than the executive, proposes a reversible initiative that the executive doubts but cannot disprove.

## 6. The memo meeting and study hall (2018, 2023)

Meetings start with a six-page narrative memo. Everyone reads it in silence. "we’ll start with a six-page narratively structured memo and we do study hall." [S009] Why not ask people to read beforehand? "they’re also bluffing like they were in college having pretended to do the reading." [S009] Writing takes time: "A good six-page memo might take two weeks to write." [S009] It is hard on the writer by design: "When you have to write in complete sentences with narrative structure, it’s really hard to hide sloppy thinking." [S009] Then the discussion wanders: "My meetings often go longer than I planned for them to because I believe in wandering." [S009]

Use it when: a hospital board or executive committee is about to approve a major capital item from a slide deck, and the case needs real scrutiny before a one-way commitment.

## 7. The customer-service phone call (2023)

The metrics said customers waited under 60 seconds for a call to be answered. Anecdotes said waits were long (inferred from S009). In a weekly business review he tested it live. "Picked up the phone, and I dialed the 1-800 number and called customer service, and we just waited in silence." [S009] It took over 10 minutes, and the data collection was fixed (inferred from S009). His rule: "you should doubt the metrics." [S009]

Use it when: dashboards show good wait times, bed turnaround, or call-center response, but patients and nurses keep complaining.

## 8. Telling investors they would probably lose (told 2020 and 2023)

He raised money with honest odds. "I told them that I thought there was a 70% chance they would lose their investment, and they did it anyway." [S007] "Told all of our early investors that I thought there was a 30% chance of success by which I just mean getting your money back" [S009]

Use it when: presenting a risky research center, startup spin-out, or new service line to a funder or ministry, and the temptation is to overstate the odds.

---

## 9. Third-party sellers: the escalated misalignment (1999 to 2018)

Letting other sellers compete on Amazon's own product pages split the company. "Internally, this was extremely controversial, with many disagreeing and some predicting this would be the beginning of a long, losing battle." [S007] "Many smart, well-intentioned Amazonians were simply not at all aligned with the direction." [S002] "The big decision set up hundreds of smaller decisions, many of which needed to be escalated to the senior team." [S002] The result, years later: "Third-party sellers are kicking our first party butt. Badly." [S004]

Use it when: an academic medical center considers opening its labs, imaging, or referral channels to outside partners, and senior staff resist on turf grounds.

---

## 10. Echo, AWS and wandering (2019 letter)

Some of his biggest bets came from no request at all. "No one asked for AWS. No one." [S004] "No customer was asking for Echo. This was definitely us wandering." [S004] "Market research doesn't help." [S004] He balances this: "Wandering is an essential counter-balance to efficiency. You need to employ both." [S004]

Use it when: a research center debates whether to fund an exploratory platform no clinician has asked for yet, versus only funding requested projects.

---

## 11. The COVID testing lab (April 2020 letter)

Early in the pandemic he argued for regular testing of everyone, and Amazon began building its own lab capacity (inferred from S005). He was candid about the odds: "We are not sure how far we will get in the relevant timeframe, but we think it's worth trying, and we stand ready to share anything we learn." [S005] "Better diagnostics help accelerate treatment and containment of this pandemic." [S005]

Use it when: a hospital weighs building in-house diagnostic or testing capacity under time pressure, with no certainty it will be ready before the need passes.

---

## 12. Who goes on the first flight (2023)

Before the first crewed New Shepard flight, he used a personal test. "if I am not ready to go, then I wouldn’t want anyone to go." [S009]

Use it when: leaders sign off on a new clinical protocol or device for patients and staff. Ask whether they would accept it for themselves or their family.
