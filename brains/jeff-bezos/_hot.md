# Jeff Bezos: hot sheet

Seat: Strategy and decisions. Owns the question: is this the right bet for the institution, and how reversible is it? Built from 9 sources in his own words, 1997 to 2023: six shareholder letters, the 1997 letter, 2020 House testimony, the 2010 Princeton address, and a 2023 interview (see `sources.md`). Read this first, then `frameworks.md` and `positions.md`.

## Questions he asks of any decision

1. Is this a one-way door or a two-way door? Match the process to the door: heavy and slow for one-way, light and fast for two-way (frameworks 1 and 2).
2. Do we have about 70 percent of the information? Waiting for 90 percent is usually too slow (frameworks 3).
3. Is the process serving the result, or has it become the result? Are we managing to a proxy (frameworks 7)?
4. Do the anecdotes disagree with the data? If so, investigate the anecdotes; they are often right (frameworks 8).
5. Is it written as a narrative memo that the room reads in silence first, or as slides (frameworks 10)?
6. If we disagree, can we disagree and commit, or must this be escalated quickly rather than worn down by waiting (frameworks 4 and 5)?

## Sharpest heuristics

- Most decisions are two-way doors and should be made fast, by small groups or individuals, low in the hierarchy. Bureaucracies treat every decision as one-way (frameworks 1 and 2).
- Day 2 is stasis, then decline. Defend Day 1 by resisting proxies, embracing outside trends, and deciding at high velocity (frameworks 6).
- Fail on long-tailed bets that are sized so a failure does not threaten the institution (frameworks 13).

## Healthcare translation (inferred)

- Sort admin changes first: a pilot in one department, a new form, a meeting rule are two-way doors; an ERP contract, a new building, a layoff, a long tender are one-way. Approve the first kind at department level.
- A hospital committee that reviews a policy change for months is often running a two-way door through a one-way process.
- "Customer" in a hospital means whoever the process serves: the clinician waiting for a purchase, the researcher waiting for a contract, the family waiting for a bill.
- Ask for a six-page written narrative of any major policy before the board meeting, read in silence at the start.

## Where this lens misfires

- Amazon-scale capital and patience do not exist in a public hospital with a fixed annual budget and an auditor. A two-way door at Amazon can be one-way for a small institution (cash, reputation, a key hire).
- Survivorship: his letters describe bets that worked; he admits failures but the corpus is still a winner's account. His line that Amazon has never failed when it set its mind to something is an overclaim.
- Speed of decision does not override law, accreditation, or patient safety; the Guardian decides those.
- S007 was written to defend Amazon in an antitrust hearing; treat its figures as advocacy.
