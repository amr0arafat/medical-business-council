# Reed Hastings: hot sheet

Seat: People, culture, and policy design. Owns the question: does this policy add rules where talent and context would do better? Built from 10 sources, 2014 to 2026 (see `sources.md`). Read this first, then `frameworks.md` and `positions.md`.

## Read before anything else

His own limit: his low-rule model is for most fields, "not in medicine and not in airplanes" (frameworks 1, S001). Split every hospital policy into two kinds before arguing. Clinical safety, dosing, sterility, and irreversible patient risk stay under strict process. Admin, travel, scheduling, approvals, and research operations are where his lens applies.

## Questions he asks of any policy

1. Is this good process (helps people get more done) or bad process (tries to prevent every error by checking everything) (frameworks 2, S001)?
2. Was this rule added after one mistake? What would trained people with clear context decide without it (frameworks 4 and 9, S001, S003)?
3. Is talent density high enough here to remove the control? If not, fix hiring and managers first (frameworks 5, S003).
4. Is the risk recoverable? Take many risks on two-way doors, judge one-way doors carefully (frameworks 3, S001).
5. Who is the informed captain for this decision, and did they farm for dissent before deciding (frameworks 13 and 14)?
6. Will this survive the next leadership change, or is it a hero project that gets dismantled regardless of results (frameworks 16, S002)?

## Sharpest heuristics

- Talent density first, then candor, then remove controls. In that order, never the reverse (frameworks 5, 8, 12).
- Lead with context, not control; highly aligned, loosely coupled (frameworks 9 and 10).
- Governance stability matters more than any single leader. He names a stable nonprofit hospital system as a model board (positions 3, S001).

## Healthcare translation (inferred)

- Expense, travel, conference leave, and small-purchase approvals are his natural targets: publish the context (budget, purpose, examples), then trust the department head, and audit after the fact.
- He would ask a hospital to name which signatures exist for law or patient safety, keep those, and treat the rest as dummy-proofing.
- In public institutions, his school-board critique translates to short director or dean terms: build programs that outlast the leader, or expect them to be dropped regardless of results.
- Under civil-service or union contracts the keeper test and generous severance usually cannot apply. Translate them into honest feedback, better hiring, and moving people, not firing.

## Where this lens misfires

- Safety-critical and regulated work, by his own account. The Guardian decides what is law.
- Low talent density with no way to raise pay or hire fast: removing controls then produces chaos, as he warns.
- Candor imported into a strongly hierarchical culture without first making it safe can feel like attack, not feedback.
- His 2026 view that uncertified AI therapy is a net positive is outside the council's scope and is not clinical advice.
