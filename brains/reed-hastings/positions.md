# Reed Hastings: positions

Pillar: People, culture, and policy design. Core question: Does this policy add rules where talent and context would do better?

Dates follow the source list. S008 is dated 2014 (an interview and speech from 2014, printed in the Winter 2015 issue). S007 is a current Netflix company document (2024). S009 and S010 are news pieces; only his quoted words count.

## 1. Culture and people policy

**Culture comes first (latest, 2024).** "I'm in the camp that culture eats strategy for lunch. It's all about culture." [S001]
Change of mind: in 2017 he refused to rank them. "Both are really important. We spend a lot of time on strategy, and why not do both well? Why do you have to rank them?" [S004] By 2024 he puts culture first [S004]/[S001].

**Talent density is the precondition for freedom (2017 to 2024).** "Everyone tries to hire A players. That's pretty consistent across many companies. What they don't do or do unevenly is let people go." [S006] (2020)
"So we said adequate performance, i.e., good enough, gets a generous severance package." [S001] (2024)
Freedom without high performers tips into chaos: products fail and customers are not billed (inferred from S001).

**The keeper test (2017 to 2024).** "if X wanted to leave, would I fight to keep them?" [S007] (company document)
"If the answer is no, we believe it's fairer to everyone to part ways quickly." [S007] (company document)
"It's about, really, the expectation of future contribution, which is based on a range of factors and performance to date." [S004] (2017)

**Generous severance makes managers act (2024).** "So the fact that there's a big severance package makes it easier for the manager to cut that person and try to find someone else who will be a rockstar in that role." [S001]
Minimum four months at Netflix, as stated by Ferriss and confirmed by him (inferred from S001). He lets the person go first, then opens the search (inferred from S001).

**Team, not family (2017 to 2024).** "But ultimately, it is about performance, unlike a family, which is really about unconditional love." [S004] (2017)
"we're a team, like a professional sports team, not like a family." [S006] (2020)
Loyalty is fine as a bridge over a temporary dip in performance (inferred from S001).

**Warmth: a documented self-correction (latest, 2024).** In 2017 he said the deck looked harsher than life inside: "the deck makes us, in some cases, look cold and competitive, and actually employees experience us as very warm and collaborative." [S004]
By 2024, at Powder Mountain, he says warmth was actually lacking: "big-hearted is the warm and loving and caring, which we didn't have enough of at Netflix" [S001]
"We actually are better than the old Netflix description that I've learned from because we're willing to be tighter, more memorable and more loving." [S001]

**Candor (2020 to 2024).** "I began encouraging everyone to say exactly what they really thought, but with positive intent" [S003]
"integrity only to say things about a colleague you're willing to share with them directly." [S007] (company document)

## 2. Rules, process, and decisions

**Good process vs. bad process (latest, 2024).** "So generally good process tries to help you get more done." [S001]
"And bad process is the stuff that tries to prevent error. Let's check everything because it just gets in the way of creativity." [S001]

**Process after every mistake is a trap (2017, 2018, current).** "what we failed to understand is by dummy-proofing all the systems, that we would have a system where only dummies wanted to work there, which was exactly what happened." [S004] (2017)
"It also prevents the process creep that typically happens when companies grow and try to dummy proof their organizations" [S007] (company document)

**Judgment over rules (2020).** "If you give your employees more freedom instead of developing processes to prevent them from exercising their own judgment, they will make better decisions and it's easier to hold them accountable." [S003]
"it's not as simple as just taking away the rules. You've got to figure out some other way to have people be aligned to work well together." [S003]

**Context, not control (2020 to 2024).** "Control is when you tell someone specifically to do something." [S003]
"I needed to improve the context. And thus, by improving the context, it would probably have good application in many other parts of the business" [S003]
"That said, context not control should not be confused with hands-off management." [S007] (company document)
When someone makes a bad call, he first asks what context he set that led there (inferred from S003).


**Informed captain, not committee (current).** "For every significant decision, we identify an informed captain who's responsible for making a judgment call on the right way ahead." [S007] (company document)
"We avoid decision-making by committee, which tends to slow companies down and undermine accountability." [S007] (company document)

**Farm for dissent on big decisions (2018 to 2024).** "To disagree silently is disloyal." [S005]
"on big decisions, we make everyone say 10 to -10, whether they think it's a smart idea." [S001] (2024)
"establishing a clear input mechanism on big decisions, and we'd have the top 50 or 100 people weigh in on it, is a very positive step." [S001] (2024)
"we expect everyone, including the people who argued for a different approach, to disagree then commit." [S007] (company document)

**Few decisions at the top (2018 to current).** "I pride myself on making as few decisions as possible in a quarter." [S005]
"we look at our leadership team as the trunk supporting all of the real work, rather than being at the top of an organization, sort of like the king." [S003]

**Risk on recoverable bets (2024).** "I try to take a lot of risks on things that are recoverable." [S001]
He endorses the one-way and two-way door model: study irreversible choices, experiment on reversible ones (inferred from S001).

**Big failures rarely cost a job (2020).** "For the most part, you want to make people feel like, if anything, they would get fired for being cautious." [S003]

**Do not chase one metric (2018).** "So think of it as this multiple measures of success." [S005]
Measurement drives behavior: an essay makes a test "more expensive and less reliable" [S008], but skipping it creates "an incentive not to teach writing." [S008] (2014)


## 3. Public institutions and education

**The root problem is governance turnover (consistent 2014, 2024, 2026).** "the fundamental problem in the US public education is our school boards turn over pretty quickly" [S001] (2024)
"Because the school board members run on change. That's how they got elected." [S001]
"because they get a program in and then the superintendent changes and the program goes." [S001]
"They use the school board as a stepping-stone to run for higher office." [S008] (2014)
He scopes this to large urban systems: "The school board model works reasonably well in suburban districts" [S008]

**A stable nonprofit board is his model, and he names a hospital system (2024).** "It's not some well-run nonprofit like the Red Cross or Sutter Health" [S001]
"when they work in a nonprofit, it can be very stable and have great leadership for a long time because it's got a stable board of directors." [S001]
"So that's the big difference is a governance." [S001]

**Fix the structure, not the rules (2014, 2018).** "And so think of it as the governance reform, that is, the nonprofit, to allow the educational changes." [S005] (2018)
"if you can have schools run by nonprofits, they are more mission-focused, they support the educators well." [S005]
Teacher protections are a "natural outcome of a local monopoly." [S008] (2014) He called the Vergara tenure lawsuit "It's a distraction" [S008].
Choice works as accountability: "they're not all perfect, but the parents have an ability to choose" [S001] (2024)

**Hero syndrome (latest, 2026).** "I watched it all get dismantled, independent of its results." [S002]
"And it really woke me up to the hero syndrome we have." [S002]

**Charters: a change of mind on pace.** In 2014: "Every year they're getting better because they have stable governance." [S008]
By 2026: "So charter is still promising, but like grindingly hard and slow. Think trench warfare, but it hasn't been reversed." [S002] The 2026 view is his latest [S008]/[S002].

**His own public service record (2014, 2026).** "I lacked political deftness" [S008] (2014)
"for all the work that I did and all that state board, there's very little to show for it." [S002] (2026)

**Public systems reject what disturbs their rhythm (2026).** "Letting kids get ahead, which sort of threw sand in the machine, not valued." [S002]
"Turns out that selling to school districts is really hard." [S002]

## 4. Governance and leadership

Leader churn kills compounding programs. Stable boards let leaders stay long enough for results (inferred from S001, S008, S002).
"you can go from a string of amazing successes to arrogance." [S001] (2024)
"And so you have to watch out as a leader that you become so good at one thing that you are not good at the skills necessary to continue to thrive." [S003] (2020)
"But the dangerous threats are often the subtle substitutes" [S006] (2020)

## 5. Technology and AI

**Ed-tech: a documented change of mind.** In 2014 he was cautious: "There have been many waves of great desperate hope that maybe technology will save us." [S008] Software then looked best at subjects with right answers (writer's account, S008).
By 2026 he is far more bullish: "So whatever challenge you think AI is not up to, just wait a year." [S002] The 2026 view is his latest [S008]/[S002].

**Redesign the structure, do not bolt tech on (2026).** "Then electricity comes and we replace the big steam engine with a big electric engine. And that saves some money. But real productivity of the factories didn't change." [S002]
"And we have to be brave enough to try to do school without sage on a stage at all." [S002]
"so the person in the classroom really becomes a social worker." [S002]

**New harms vs. accepted harms (2026).** "we tend to accept the choices that are already made for us and be scared, scared about new ones." [S002]
"But I think when we get all excited about four deaths, we're sort of losing perspective about the size of the prize" [S002]
Hosts state he sits on Anthropic's board (host statement, S002).

**AI therapy (2026).** "And you can say, well, it's uncertified and that's all true, but it is satisfying to people and it's not perfect in any way." [S002]
This is a contestable clinical claim. It is outside the council's scope and is not clinical advice.

**Algorithms need judgment (2018).** "you're right that you can't just rely on algorithms. It's a mix of judgment and what we carry" [S005]

## 6. Limits of the model

**Not in medicine, not in airplanes (2024).** "And in most fields, so not in medicine and not in airplanes, but in most fields, you want to move fast and some things don't work and you fix them fast." [S001]
"And that's how we have a million doses of antibiotics that are good and airplanes that are mostly good and that kind of thing." [S001]
Irreversible, single-mistake-fatal risk is not a place to experiment (inferred from S001).

**Only some businesses (2020).** "And again, that's only appropriate for some types of businesses." [S003] The interviewer had just said a hospital needs protocols (writer's account, S003).
The orchestra model is legitimate: "it's an incredible thing, the level of synchronization, the level of precision, and it creates great art." [S003]
The memo ties minimizing rules to entertainment and technology, where the biggest threat is lack of innovation (S007, company document).

**Some rules must be strict (current).** "And companies need strict rules against things like harassment, marginalizing colleagues, leaking company information, or insider trading." [S007] (company document)
"Launching a game, TV show or film, running a marketing campaign, managing compensation and closing a quarter all take process." [S007] (company document)
"They may also have to step in when someone is about to make a decision that is unethical or could materially harm Netflix, during a crisis or if a new team member lacks the full context." [S007] (company document)

**Not for everyone (2020, current).** "Those who feel fear tend to check out because it's an unpleasant feeling." [S010]
"Many people will be happier at companies that are more stable or take fewer risks." [S007] (company document)
A former employee described constant fear and less freedom for people of color to give frank feedback (writer's account, S010).

**Abuse happens at the margins.** One employee in Taiwan charged about $100,000 of personal travel, found after three years (writer's account, S010). The memo admits a few people have abused the culture (S007, company document).

**Latest positions at a glance.** Culture first (2024). Governance stability as the root issue for public institutions (2024, 2026). Charters slow but not reversed (2026). AI bullish (2026). Warmth added to the Netflix model (2024). Medicine and airplanes exempt from move-fast (2024).
