# Reed Hastings: stories

Anecdotes he tells, kept close to his words. Each ends with when to use it on a hospital or research-center decision.

## 1. Pure Software and process creep

His first company grew and he added a rule after every mistake.
"As we hired new employees, a few did stupid stuff, leading to errors that cost the company money. Each time this happened, I put a process in place to prevent that mistake from occurring again." [S003]
One trigger was a $700-a-night stay at the Willard hotel, which led to a travel policy with spending limits and management approval (inferred from S003).
"Policies and control processes became so foundational to our work that those who were great at coloring within the lines were promoted, while many creative mavericks felt stifled and went to work elsewhere." [S003]
"We had become increasingly effective and decreasingly creative." [S003]
"But we had selected and conditioned our employees to follow process, not to think freshly or shift fast." [S003]
The market moved from C++ to Java and Pure was sold to its largest competitor in 1997 (S003, S005).

Use it when: a committee wants a new administrative rule after one incident, and you need to ask whether the rule fixes a person problem or burdens everyone.

## 2. The overworked, conflict-avoidant founder

"I was coding all night, trying to be CEO in the day, and once in a while would squeeze in a shower. And it turns out that's not a good recipe for leading a large group of people." [S004]
"I was conflict-avoidant. People would become upset if I addressed them directly with a problem, so I would try to work around issues when they came up." [S003]
He went around an engineer named Aki to outside engineers, and Aki confronted him (inferred from S003).

Use it when: a leader is solving a staff problem by working around a person instead of giving direct feedback.

## 3. The marriage counselor and the origin of candor

He kept saying family came first while working all night.
"the counselor was very good at getting me to see that I was a systemic liar." [S001]
"And that misalignment with how we act and what we say can be very hurtful." [S001]
"So since then I've been really committed to trying to reflect and say the honest thing." [S001]

Use it when: an institution's stated values (patient first, staff wellbeing) do not match how it actually behaves.

## 4. The 2001 layoffs and talent density

After the dot-com crash Netflix cut about a third of staff, keeping 80 of 120 (inferred from S003).
"Some were great colleagues and friends but did adequate work rather than great work." [S003]
"The entire office felt like it was filled with people who were madly in love with their work." [S003]
Patty McCord asked whether it felt like being in love (S003).

Use it when: a hospital must shrink a department and is deciding whether to cut evenly or keep the strongest team.

## 5. The vacation and expense policies

"Sure, that's a big symbolic one, vacation, because most people, in practice, do that, anyway." [S005]
"And of course, I am a strong role model of taking lots of vacation. And I do some of my best thinking when I'm on vacation." [S003]
"The number of hours that you work in a day is also not formally regulated. People get an approximate social understanding and that seems to work pretty well." [S003]
On expenses: "And we ask people to do what you would think is best for the company. We don't give them any more guidelines than that." [S004]
"How should I travel: Business, or coach, or by bus?" [S004]
The memo sets the vacation policy at two words and the expense policy at five (S007, company document).
The cost: an employee in Taiwan charged about $100,000 of personal travel, found after three years (writer's account, S010).

Use it when: reviewing approval chains for leave, travel, or purchases in non-clinical administration.

## 6. The culture deck goes public

He started after the 2002 IPO, walked new hires through it, sent it to every candidate, then posted it online around 2007 to 2008 (S004).
"It's not very pretty, it's not very highly designed, doesn't look like it's an external marketing piece, but that authenticity, really, people liked in the outside world" [S004]
"The unexpected benefit was many people became candidates for us, because they loved that" [S004]

Use it when: recruiting staff or leaders, and deciding whether to state the real expectations up front.

## 7. Qwikster

In 2011 he split DVD and streaming into two services.
"We didn't do much farming for dissent in those days. So I was messianic, convinced this is the right move, looking around the corner." [S001]
"That clouded, essentially, my judgment about how normal people consuming Netflix would feel about it." [S003]
"it turned out that lots of people had severe doubts, but they didn't know the other executives had doubts." [S001]
"we realized that if all the peers had been talking, then they would have all ganged up on me and prevented Qwikster." [S003]
Netflix lost 800,000 subscribers in four months and he canceled the spinoff (writer's account, S008). The stock fell about 75 percent that year (S003).
The fix: "on these big decisions everybody has to write down in public in a shared document, how they feel about the idea and their judgment about it." [S003]

Use it when: a chief executive is pushing a big restructuring and senior staff are quiet in meetings.

## 8. The informed captain

"For every significant decision, we identify an informed captain who's responsible for making a judgment call on the right way ahead." [S007] (company document)
"We avoid decision-making by committee, which tends to slow companies down and undermine accountability." [S007] (company document)
The captain reflects publicly afterward on what worked and what did not (S007, company document).

Use it when: a decision has stalled in committee and no one person owns the outcome.

## 9. Blockbuster and the green crystals

Under attack from Blockbuster, Netflix spent effort on visible counterattacks like banner ads and used DVD sales, about 20 percent of effort wasted (inferred from S001). He compares it to dish soap sold on green crystals.
"And in hindsight, it's because we, ourselves, needed to believe in the green crystals." [S001]
"And we could have done better if we had had the discipline and confidence to just execute on the core better." [S001]

Use it when: leaders want a showy new initiative while core services (wait times, billing, discharge) are weak.

## 10. Context, not control: the addiction ad

A team made an ad framed around addiction. He saw it as a symptom of vague context he had set, not a reason to overrule (inferred from S003).
"I needed to improve the context. And thus, by improving the context, it would probably have good application in many other parts of the business" [S003]

Use it when: a manager is about to override a subordinate's decision instead of clarifying the goal.

## 11. Roy Romer and hero syndrome

Roy Romer led LA Unified for a record seven years with gains, then it was dismantled within five years (inferred from S002).
"I watched it all get dismantled, independent of its results." [S002]
"So we see this cycle of rise and fall." [S002]

Use it when: a public hospital's reform depends on one director, and a new appointee may undo it.

## 12. DreamBox and the grammar of schooling

Districts asked DreamBox to turn off letting kids get ahead (inferred from S002).
"Letting kids get ahead, which sort of threw sand in the machine, not valued." [S002]
"an early lesson in sort of the depth and strength of the grammar of schooling that we have." [S002]

Use it when: an innovation works for patients or researchers but clashes with the institution's scheduling or budget rhythm.

## 13. Steam to electricity

"Then electricity comes and we replace the big steam engine with a big electric engine. And that saves some money. But real productivity of the factories didn't change." [S002]
Gains came only when factories were redesigned around the new power (inferred from S002).

Use it when: buying AI or digital tools and asking whether workflows will change or only the tool.

## 14. Peace Corps in Swaziland

He taught math. "There were no rules at all. Just use your initiative." [S008]

Use it when: deciding how much latitude to give trainees or junior staff in non-clinical roles.

## 15. The keeper and the fish

The keeper test is named for his father saying a fish he caught as a child was a keeper (S001; S007, company document).
"And so we wanted to fill the company with people that we would fight to keep." [S001]

Use it when: running a performance review and asking whether each role holder is someone you would fight to keep.

## 16. Negative feedback still hurts

A vice president told him he was not a good listener (writer's account, S010).
"even, you know, at my level of success, it hurts because I thought I had done better in that dimension." [S010]
"But I remember that feedback is like exercise." [S010]

Use it when: setting up upward feedback for senior leaders who resist it.
