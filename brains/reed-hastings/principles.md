# Reed Hastings: Principles

Short rules he states directly. One line each: the principle, then the quote and source.

## Rules and judgment

- Prefer principles to rules. "I'm not a big person for rules. I'm a big person for principles and understanding and experiences." [S006]
- Invest in judgment, not rules. "The basic thing that we're trying to get through is investing in the human capacity, not so much in rules, but in good judgment." [S003]
- Freedom improves decisions and accountability. "If you give your employees more freedom instead of developing processes to prevent them from exercising their own judgment, they will make better decisions and it's easier to hold them accountable." [S003]
- Share information so people can decide. "You get better outcomes when employees have the information and freedom to make decisions for themselves." [S007] (company document)
- Judgment cannot be made objective. "judgment is not objective. Judgment is judgment." [S003]
- Think from first principles, not from the process. "because we had a bunch of people who valued following the process rather than the first-principle thinking." [S004]
- Freedom is a deliberate risk. "It's risky trusting employees as much as we do, giving them as much freedom as we do. But it's essential in creative companies where you have much greater risk from lack of innovation." [S010]

## Where rules stay

- Medicine and aviation are exceptions to moving fast. "And in most fields, so not in medicine and not in airplanes, but in most fields, you want to move fast and some things don't work and you fix them fast." [S001]
- The edge-of-chaos style fits only some businesses. "And again, that's only appropriate for some types of businesses." [S003]
- Without a system, growth breeds chaos. "I understood that as a company grows, if you don't manage it with policies or control processes, the organization is likely to descend into chaos." [S003]
- Keep strict rules on conduct. "And companies need strict rules against things like harassment, marginalizing colleagues, leaking company information, or insider trading." [S007] (company document)
- Context is not hands-off. "That said, context not control should not be confused with hands-off management." [S007] (company document)

## Risk and innovation

- Punish caution, not bold failure. "For the most part, you want to make people feel like, if anything, they would get fired for being cautious." [S003]
- Firing for big failures should be rare. "But you're not going to support a lot of innovation if you do that very often." [S003]
- Take recoverable risks. "I try to take a lot of risks on things that are recoverable." [S001]
- Do not get too good at one thing. "And so you have to watch out as a leader that you become so good at one thing that you are not good at the skills necessary to continue to thrive." [S003]
- Prepare early for shifts. "you want to be preparing for that long in advance." [S006]
- Watch for quiet threats. "But the dangerous threats are often the subtle substitutes" [S006]
- Beware of overlearning. "So, sometimes, you overlearn one lesson that, 10 years later, there's a different answer to." [S006]
- Success breeds arrogance. "you can go from a string of amazing successes to arrogance." [S001]

## People and talent

- Culture comes first. "I'm in the camp that culture eats strategy for lunch. It's all about culture." [S001]
- The core management question. "How do you get human beings to work well together and accomplish amazing things?" [S001]
- Fill the place with people you would fight to keep. "And so we wanted to fill the company with people that we would fight to keep." [S001]
- If the keeper answer is no, part ways quickly. "If the answer is no, we believe it's fairer to everyone to part ways quickly." [S007] (company document)
- Let people go with dignity. "we wanted to let people go in the way that we would want to be let go if we're on the other side" [S001]
- No brilliant jerks. "we don't tolerate the jerks, brilliant or not." [S003]
- Talent and teamwork both count. "So you have to be both talented and good at teamwork." [S003]
- Need people who handle context, not rigidity. "people who get the overall context of what's going on and don't need a lot of rigidity to get good results." [S001]
- References must be close to you. "the key is that the reference has to be closer to me or someone I know than to the subject." [S001]
- Reward balance, not burnout. "what we try to do is reward people who show good balance and accomplish a lot and are trying to run the marathon in terms of sustainability." [S003]
- Not everyone fits, and that is fine. "Many people will be happier at companies that are more stable or take fewer risks." [S007] (company document)

## Candor and dissent

- Silent disagreement is disloyal. "To disagree silently is disloyal." [S001]
- Put your view in writing. "It's not OK to let some decision go through without saying your piece, and typically, writing it down." [S005]
- Build a formal input channel. "establishing a clear input mechanism on big decisions, and we'd have the top 50 or 100 people weigh in on it, is a very positive step." [S001]
- Debate is curiosity, not yelling. "You know, it's really curiosity drawing people out." [S005]
- Not all opinions weigh the same. "Of course, not all opinions are created equal" [S007] (company document)
- Serve the organization, not the boss. "And we try to reorient people to the goal is to serve the organization." [S003]
- Say the honest thing. "So since then I've been really committed to trying to reflect and say the honest thing." [S001]
- Words must match actions. "And that misalignment with how we act and what we say can be very hurtful." [S001]
- Lead by example. "if I can set a good example of being honest and open and curious, then we will attract people like that" [S006]
- Feedback still hurts, and it helps. "when someone gives me negative feedback, it still hurts." [S006]

## Leadership and decisions

- Senior leaders should decide little. "priding ourselves on how few, not how many, decisions senior leaders make." [S007] (company document)
- No decision by committee. "We avoid decision-making by committee, which tends to slow companies down and undermine accountability." [S007] (company document)
- Improve the culture, do not preserve it. "And I always say the goal is not to preserve it, it's to improve it." [S003]
- Focus on few things. "I've always thought the people who really focus on a few things will get the most done." [S001]
- Do not outrun your customers. "sometimes you can be so strategic in long term that you don't bring along the customers with you." [S001]
- Use multiple measures of success. "So think of it as this multiple measures of success." [S005]
- Metrics need judgment. "you're right that you can't just rely on algorithms. It's a mix of judgment and what we carry" [S005]
- Keep doubt alive. "Take time every day to examine different beliefs, and stay skeptical. Doubt is an essential counterbalance to story" [S009]

## Public institutions

- Fix governance before programs. "So that's the big difference is a governance." [S001]
- Stable governance compounds. "Every year they're getting better because they have stable governance." [S008]
- Attack the structure, not the protections. Teacher protections are a "natural outcome of a local monopoly." [S008]
- Measurement shapes behavior. Not testing writing creates "an incentive not to teach writing." [S008]
- Make the front line's life easier to get adopted. "if you want to make money selling to school districts, make teachers' lives easier." [S002]
- Respect opponents. "A public advocate needs a thick skin and a respect for opposing arguments" [S008]
- Do not freeze on new risks while ignoring old ones. "we tend to accept the choices that are already made for us and be scared, scared about new ones." [S002]
