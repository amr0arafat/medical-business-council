## Why the council landed here

- Four of the seven signatures trace to an old internal memo, not to law, and nobody could name what they had caught. (Musk, five-step algorithm: question every requirement)
- The limiting step is the paper file moving between buildings, not the reviews themselves; e-approval removes most of the 34 days. (Grove, production principle)
- A two-department pilot is reversible in a week; a hospital-wide change in an accreditation year is not. (Bezos, Type 1 and Type 2 decisions)
- Delays cost research grants with spending deadlines, and the pilot targets exactly those units. (Gates, cost per outcome)

## Where they fought

**Delete now or pilot first?** Musk and Hastings opened on B. **Can the financial controller go?** Musk argued it adds nothing for small purchases. The Guardian held that the state financial rules for public entities require it. Musk conceded. Resolved: the controller stays and signs electronically within 48 hours.

## Conditions

1. Median approval time under 10 days in the pilot units by day 90 (baseline 34).
2. Zero new audit findings on a 10 percent sample of pilot purchases.
3. University council paper filed by day 60.

## 30-day plan

1. Week 1: director signs the pilot decision; internal audit agrees the sampling plan (director's office, internal audit).
2. Weeks 2 to 4: IT configures e-approval for the two units; department heads trained (IT, purchasing).
3. Day 30: first data review with the director, the controller, and both department heads.

**Approval path:** hospital director (pilot), then university council (hospital-wide).
**Cheapest pilot:** the pilot itself; if IT slips, run it on paper with single-building routing for 30 days.
**Success metric:** median approval days, 34 to under 10, by day 90.
**Kill criteria:** any material audit finding, or a median above 20 days at day 60. Revert to the old chain for those units.
**Revisit:** day 90, with cycle time, audit sample results, and order volume by threshold.
